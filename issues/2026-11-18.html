EscalatingIran war · day 264 (began Feb 28)

Iran hits Bahrain a third time, the US strikes Qeshm, and Hormuz burns again

What happened

Since Monday: the Revolutionary Guards fired drones Tuesday at a US radar at Isa Air Base in Bahrain, citing "continued violations of Iranian airspace" by US drones. That was Iran's third attack on US targets in Bahrain in five days, after Nov 13 and Nov 15. CENTCOM said every drone was intercepted, with no damage or injuries (Iran International; NPR). Hours later US jets struck an IRGC missile-launch site on Qeshm Island, in what a US official called self-defense. A local Iranian official said no residential or vital areas were hit (Gulf News). Iran's army and the IRGC each claimed to have shot down a US drone over Hormuz; neither claim is confirmed. Kuwait's air defenses engaged drones Tuesday night, and Iran claimed hits on Patriot batteries at Ali Al-Salem, which is also unconfirmed. Tuesday evening ADNOC said one of its vessels was attacked in the strait, with one crew member missing and 11 injured. Oman evacuated 24 people from a burning tanker off Musandam, and the IRGC Navy claimed a supertanker and an LNG carrier hit mines (Gulf News). Foreign Minister Araghchi met Oman's Badr Albusaidi in Muscat Tuesday. The readouts mentioned only "safeguarding the security of maritime passages" and announced no Hormuz arrangement. Qatar said it would meet Iranian officials the same day. In Manila, asked about a US intelligence report that Iran is planning a "dirty bomb" attack in Washington, Trump said Iran would "pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid." Officials said nothing points to an imminent attack.

Why it matters

Last week's reported strike pause depended on Iran not provoking the US. Three attacks on Bahrain and the first US strike on Iranian soil in weeks suggest that condition is already slipping, the night before Iran is raised at Shenzhen.

How each side sees it

Tehran: we will not retreat

Judiciary chief Mohseni-Eje'i: "Our armed forces have not retreated in front of the enemy and will not retreat." The IRGC's spokesman accused Bahrain of hiding inside a US "security bubble."

Via Iran International (diaspora, anti-regime) · Gulf News (UAE, pro-government)
Washington: responding to aggression

A US military official said the Qeshm strike "was in response to IRGC aggression targeting US personnel and assets." Rubio condemned the attacks in a call with Bahrain's foreign minister.

Via NPR (mainstream) · Arab News (Saudi, government-aligned)
Gulf mediators: this endangers a deal

Qatar's Majed Al-Ansari called on "all parties to exercise restraint" and called the escalation an "extremely serious" threat to a deal. The GCC's Jasem Albudaiwi called Iran's attack a "blatant challenge" to peace efforts.

Via Arab News (Saudi, government-aligned) · The National (UAE, state-owned)

EscalatingUkraine war / peace track

A span of the Crimean Bridge falls; Russia answers with missiles at Kyiv's bridges

What happened

Since Monday: Ukrainian Flamingo missiles and drones struck the Kerch (Crimean) Bridge two days running. One arch and the road span beneath it collapsed. The road section is out of service, and rail runs on one track. Zelensky called it the "seventh phase of an operation" weeks in the making (Kyiv Independent). Russia's defense ministry claimed 2,049 drones intercepted in 24 hours, the largest attack of the war (UNITED24, a Ukrainian government outlet, citing Russian state TV). A later ministry figure of 1,441 drones in one night doesn't match it (ABC Australia). Ukraine also hit the Ilsky refinery in Krasnodar and Gazprom's Salavat plant in Bashkortostan; local officials deny major damage at Salavat (The Moscow Times). Overnight into Tuesday, Russian ballistic missiles targeted Kyiv's Northern and Southern bridges. At least seven people were killed across Ukraine, and 50,000 lost power. At Tuesday's Ramstein meeting Zelensky asked for 40 more F-16s, and Denmark pledged €645 million including €200 million for air defense (Interfax-Ukraine). He also announced talks in Berlin this week with US envoys and Europeans. Kushner said Moscow is open to Europeans joining the negotiations. The prisoner swap, agreed in Abu Dhabi, could now involve up to 1,300 people but still hasn't happened.

Why it matters

The bridge is Russia's main overland supply link to Crimea. Hitting it on the eve of Shenzhen shows Kyiv using leverage on the ground while the great powers talk.

NewIsraeli election

Eisenkot's bloc takes the Knesset speakership with Arab votes

What happened

Since Monday: after a six-hour filibuster by Netanyahu's bloc, the Knesset elected Yashar's Matan Kahana speaker Tuesday night, 66–52, over Likud's Ofir Katz. Kahana's votes came from Eisenkot's 48-seat bloc plus the Joint List (11) and Ra'am (7), and nine deputy speakers were elected, including Ahmad Tibi (Times of Israel). Ynet reports that Eisenkot's bloc agreed not to advance repeal of the judicial-overhaul laws until Monday in exchange for ending the filibuster. Likud's election appeal didn't come today: the Jerusalem District Court gave it four extra days, so the deadline is now about Nov 22. Maariv, citing a Likud source, reports that Netanyahu serving second in a rotation "cannot be ruled out" (Jerusalem Post).

Why it matters

It's the first time the new majority has proven itself on a floor vote, and it did so only with Arab parties. That shows both its strength and the attack line Likud will use against any government it forms.

Oil slides on returning Hormuz flows, then bounces on Gulf attacks; diesel dips under $6

DevelopingCOP31

COP31's first draft goes big on fossil fuels and draws fire from both sides

What happened

Since Monday: Chris Bowen, Australia's COP31 president of negotiations, released an 18-page, 144-paragraph draft cover decision Monday. It calls for an accelerated fossil-fuel phase-out and an end to inefficient fossil subsidies. It endorses the "35-by-35" target of 35% electricity in final energy by 2035, net zero for developed countries by 2030, and tripled adaptation finance of at least $120 billion a year by 2035 (ABC Australia; Down To Earth). Brazil's voluntary fossil transition roadmap is only "welcomed," in paragraph 140, with no follow-up process (Climate Home News). Bowen calls it a compilation, not agreed text, and promises "at least one more draft" this week. The Arab Group, the Like-Minded Developing Countries (including China, India and Saudi Arabia) and Russia said it crosses "red lines" on electrification, the roadmap, methane and new mandates. The EU's Wopke Hoekstra called it a "shopping list" that "needs significant change," objecting to the finance, trade and carbon-capture language while backing faster electrification. On Tuesday Denmark became the fifth country to publish its own fossil-transition roadmap. In the US, EPA's partial repeal of the 2024 power-plant greenhouse-gas rule took effect Monday. A challenge by 21 states is pending at the D.C. Circuit, with no stay.

Why it matters

The text is the strongest on fossil fuels in years, and it's under attack from petrostates and from the EU at once. Unless the next draft shrinks dramatically, the talks risk ending Friday with only procedural decisions.
