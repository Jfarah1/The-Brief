Iran hits Bahrain and the tanker lanes; Trump points to strikes "after the new year"

What happened

Since Friday: at his post-summit news conference Trump said Iran wants a deal, but not one "that's acceptable to me," and called reports of an Iranian dirty-bomb plot in Washington a "rumor" (CBS News). He told Israel's Channel 12 he's "not rushing to make any decisions on Iran." In a Fox News interview Sunday he said he's inclined to order strikes after the new year if talks fail, and warned that Iranian ships challenging the US blockade would end up "at the bottom of the sea" (Fox via Jerusalem Post and Israel National News). On Saturday Bahrain said an Iranian strike hit a civilian industrial facility on Sitra Island, starting a fire and injuring two people, one Bahraini and one Pakistani (Kuwait Times). CBS counts it as the fourth Iranian attempt on Bahrain in about a week. The IRGC claimed it destroyed two US Patriot radars in retaliation for a US strike on a radar on Hengam Island; CENTCOM hasn't confirmed either. The UAE and Saudi Arabia condemned the attack. In Hormuz the IRGC says two more tankers hit mines Sunday. Al Jazeera counts at least 25 tankers hit since Nov 14, and UKMTO counted 263 transits Nov 12–18, down 15% on the week. Iran's foreign ministry says it will use "all available tools" to break the blockade. Deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi says the "window of diplomacy is still open" (Iran International). Qatari mediation continues.

Why it matters

Iran is turning up pressure on Gulf hosts of US forces and on shipping while Washington's decision slips into 2027. The longer the blockade holds without a deal, the more these incidents risk a clash at sea that forces the timing.

EscalatingUkraine war / peace track

Poland confirms three Russian cruise missiles on its soil; NATO meets today

What happened

Since Friday: Polish prosecutors now say all three objects that came down Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, not two missiles and a drone. They landed near Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz, with no casualties. A prosecutor said one recovered missile was made in the fourth quarter of 2026 (Militarnyi; Kyiv Post). One fell about 35 km from a Coalition of the Willing exercise. A Swedish Gripen was cleared to shoot one down but didn't fire, with poor weather cited (AeroTime). Poland didn't invoke NATO's Article 4. It asked for more Eastern Sentry assets, including AWACS and F-35s. NATO's Mark Rutte, in Warsaw Saturday, called it a "grave violation" and pledged to defend "every inch" of Poland (Reuters via US News). The North Atlantic Council meets in Brussels today (ProtoThema). Trump said Putin landed "a couple of little missiles, by mistake" (Newsweek via Al Jazeera). Friday's Russian barrage of 110-plus missiles and 400-plus drones killed at least nine people and hit three Kyiv bridges (Kyiv Independent). Strikes continued over the weekend, killing at least three people in the Kyiv region Sunday. Zelensky called US delays on Patriot deliveries "difficult to understand," citing 165 Russian missiles in one week. The UK and Ukraine signed a deal to build long-range drones in Britain. The prisoner swap of 1,200–1,300 per side hasn't happened yet.

Why it matters

Missiles landing on NATO soil with no interception and no Article 4 call is the kind of response analysts say Moscow is probing. The Brussels meeting will show whether the alliance treats this as an accident or a pattern.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded) · NV.ua (Ukrainian, carrying ISW)

EscalatingSaudi–Houthi war

Coalition pounds Hodeidah port; Houthis warn airlines off Saudi airspace

What happened

Since Friday: after telling civilians to leave, the Saudi-led coalition launched a "special operation" on Hodeidah port Friday, with strikes also in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz. Al Jazeera's Sanaa correspondent called them "the heaviest air strikes that we have witnessed since the conflict began." The UN says Hodeidah handles about 70% of Yemen's commercial imports and voiced concern about humanitarian supply (The National). Yemen's government says the port is still operating. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree told airlines to avoid Saudi airspace except near Mecca and Medina, and told shipping companies and workers to leave Saudi Red Sea ports, including Jeddah (Dawn). The Houthis claim hits on the al-Shuqaiq power station and Jazan port; the coalition says it intercepted two missiles over Jazan, with debris at Shuqaiq, and three more early Saturday. Houthi-run media say 41 people were killed in 24 hours; that's unverified. Xinhua (Chinese state media) reported 5 dead and 18 injured across Yemen Sunday. No strike on Jeddah has been claimed. On the ground, the Houthis are reported about 60 km from Aden.

Why it matters

Hitting Yemen's main import port raises the humanitarian stakes sharply, and the Houthi warnings now reach Saudi civil aviation and Jeddah. Western officials told Reuters Riyadh expects the campaign to last into 2027.

How each side sees it

Coalition: cutting the weapons pipeline

Spokesman Turki al-Malki says the strikes target "smuggling operations and the transport of weapons and ballistic missiles."

ResolvedCOP31

COP31 ends with no deal, the first collapse since Copenhagen

What happened

Since Friday: talks in Antalya ran roughly two days past their Friday close and broke up late Sunday local time without a cover decision. Reports call it the longest COP ever and the first to end without agreement since 2009 (Seoul Economic Daily). The second draft had dropped all fossil-fuel language after pressure from Saudi Arabia and other producers. Later drafts added carbon-capture and electrification targets. The EU, Panama, the Marshall Islands, Norway and Vanuatu objected formally (Middle East Eye). COP President Murat Kurum told the plenary "these decisions have not been adopted." Reports differ on whether any side items passed. An electrification pledge was signed outside the formal process; Chris Bowen, who chaired negotiations, cited more than 90 countries (Asharq Al-Awsat), while The Conversation reports more than 150. COP32 is in Addis Ababa in 2027.

Why it matters

Two years after COP28's call to transition away from fossil fuels, the process couldn't agree to repeat it. The consensus rule, which lets any bloc block a text, is now the main issue.

How each side sees it

EU and climate-vulnerable states: no deal beats a bad one

Ireland's Darragh O'Brien, for the EU: no "Antalya pact that does not even mention fossil fuel." Panama's Juan Carlos Navarro: "held hostage by petroleum producers."

Via CBS News (mainstream) · Middle East Eye (UK-based, left)
The presidency: consensus cuts both ways

Kurum: "we did not impose this text on anyone." Bowen: "you also need consensus to change consensus."

Via Asharq Al-Awsat (Saudi-owned)
Critics: producers won

Via The Conversation (academic) · Asharq Al-Awsat
