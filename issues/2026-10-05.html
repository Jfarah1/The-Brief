EscalatingSaudi–Houthi war · truce collapsed in July

Houthis claim strike on Aramco site near Riyadh; Saudis hit back at Sanaa

What happened

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said a "military operation with ballistic missiles and drones" struck an Aramco facility in Riyadh and set it on fire. Reporters saw flames and smoke near a site south of the capital. Saudi authorities did not confirm the attack caused the fire, and Saudi aircraft then struck Houthi-held Sanaa.

Why it matters

The 2022 Saudi–Houthi truce is gone. With the Iran war already squeezing Gulf exports, a second front against Saudi energy sites matters for oil prices, and it makes any reserve release harder to hold.

How each side sees it

Houthi / "Axis" framing

The strike was retaliation for stepped-up Saudi air raids, part of a policy of "blockade for blockade" and "escalation for escalation" until Riyadh stops bombing and lifts port and airport restrictions.

Via Al Jazeera · amplified by RT (Russian state media)
Saudi-led coalition

Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki called the claims "misleading," an "illusory victory" meant to distract from Houthi losses to Yemeni government forces on the ground.

Via Daily Pakistan · Al Jazeera
Watch: independent damage assessment or satellite imagery of the site, and whether Aramco reports any output disruption when markets open.

NewUkraine peace track

US eyes Abu Dhabi talks on a limited "energy truce"; report says a Lukoil deal is on the table

What happened

Washington is reportedly organizing Ukraine–Russia talks this month, most likely in Abu Dhabi. The proposal is a phased approach that starts with a mutual halt on strikes against power plants and refineries, plus an Egypt-mediated deal on Black Sea shipping. Meanwhile Zelensky said Kyiv will step up refinery strikes in response to expanded Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure. A New York Times report, picked up widely, says the talks now include a multibillion-dollar purchase of Lukoil's global assets that Putin raised with Witkoff and Kushner on Sept 5.

Why it matters

Ukraine's refinery strikes are one reason Russia has stopped exporting diesel. A deal on energy strikes is therefore also a deal on the world diesel shortage driving US prices.

How each side sees it

Administration / pragmatist view

Small, verifiable deals (no energy strikes, safe grain shipping) build toward a broader ceasefire. US negotiators reportedly saw the Lukoil deal as a way to build trust with the Kremlin and bring down global energy prices. The reporting found no sign Witkoff or Kushner would profit personally.

Via Liga.net · Kyiv Post
Critics / conflict-of-interest view

The consortium includes Todd Boehly, who gave $2M to pro-Trump groups, and two Gulf investment groups with past business ties to the Kushner and Witkoff families. Critics say tying a peace deal to a sanctions-lifting asset sale rewards Moscow and blurs public and private interests.

Via The Moscow Times (exiled, anti-Kremlin) · Ukrainska Pravda
Watch: a date and venue for the Abu Dhabi talks, and whether Treasury signals any Lukoil sanctions relief.

DevelopingBrazil election · runoff Oct 25 if needed

Brazil votes: Lula seeks a fourth term against Flávio Bolsonaro

What happened

Brazilians voted Sunday in a race that polls showed as essentially tied between President Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, the former president's eldest son. If neither clears 50%, the top two meet in an Oct 25 runoff. The results were not final when this issue was built. Wednesday's issue will cover them.

Why it matters

Latin America's largest economy is choosing between closer alignment with Washington (Bolsonaro) and Lula's "sovereignty" posture, with critical minerals and China trade at stake. Whoever wins inherits a debt load projected above 86% of GDP and interest costs near 8% of GDP.

How each side sees it

Lula camp

He's running on continuity and Brazilian sovereignty, keeping balanced ties with both the US and China. He holds a big lead with women, the key swing group.

Via Al Jazeera · CFR
Bolsonaro camp

He promises to rein in inflation and spending, and an adviser says he would put in a debt ceiling. He wants closer alignment with Washington.

Via CNBC · Foreign Policy

Skeptic's note: Foreign Policy argues that neither candidate has a credible plan for Brazil's growing economic dependence on Beijing.

NewFed tightening cycle

Hiring stalls at 29,000 jobs a month after the Fed raised rates

What happened

Payrolls rose by just 29,000 in September, against forecasts of 84–90k. Unemployment ticked up to 4.2%. July was revised to a loss of 10,000, and July and August together lost 60,000 jobs in revisions. Average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% on the month and 3% on the year, likely below inflation. Stocks rose, and CME futures now give only ~17% odds of another hike at the late-October meeting. The Fed's range is 3.75–4.00%.

Why it matters

The Fed raised rates in September to fight energy-driven inflation. With 3% wage growth trailing prices and gas and diesel at records, this is where the "stagflation" label stops being rhetoric. Even so, the 10-year yield rose Friday to 5.28%. Bond markets still fear the inflation more than they welcome the slowdown.

How each side sees it

"The Fed overshot"

Raising rates into an oil supply shock was a mistake. Higher rates can't produce diesel, but they can cost jobs. This report is the first warning.

Via 24/7 Wall St. · TipRanks
"Don't read too much into payrolls"

The household survey looks much stronger than the headline. ZeroHedge's headline: "Payrolls Plunge… But Employment Soars." Unemployment is still low by historical standards, and inflation is the bigger threat.

Via ZeroHedge (right-libertarian, markets) · Fox Business
Watch: the September CPI release and Fed speakers this week. Any official who calls the September hike "one and done" is a signal.

DevelopingEnergy crisis · Iran war day 218

G7 opens the taps: 100 million barrels, diesel first

What happened

Macron announced Friday that G7 countries and partners will release up to 100 million barrels through the IEA over four months. Roughly 50M will be European diesel and 50M crude, with a "substantial" diesel release in the first 20 days. European gasoil futures fell ~4%, and Brent briefly dipped under $100 before closing near $102.70. US retail diesel hit a record of about $6.50/gal.

Why it matters

The shortage has three causes at once. The Iran war has cut Gulf exports from 23.3 to about 21.8 million barrels a day. Ukraine's refinery strikes have stopped Russian diesel exports. And China has halted diesel exports. A reserve release fills the gap for a while; it doesn't add refining capacity.

How each side sees it

"It will help"

A release this size should "put some downward pressure on prices, particularly global diesel prices," especially in the first 20 days, which fall in Europe's harvest-season peak in demand.

Hamad Hussain (Capital Economics) via Al Jazeera · OilPrice.com
"A band-aid"

The release "doesn't deal with the fundamental problem that the global supply remains lower than normal." Estimates put the effect at about $0.25/gal off diesel, roughly half of the recent spike by Goldman's math.

Neil Atkinson via Al Jazeera · Discovery Alert · Nairametrics
Watch: whether Brent holds above $100 after the Aramco strike, and how quickly the first diesel actually reaches the market.

NewSCOTUS · October Term 2026

Can a Colorado county sue Exxon over climate change? The justices hear it today.

What happened

Boulder County is suing Suncor and ExxonMobil under Colorado law. It says the companies misled the public about climate risks and should help pay for heat, drought, wildfire and flood costs. The companies, backed by the Trump administration, say federal law and the Clean Air Act preempt the suit. There's also a threshold question: does the Court even have jurisdiction before the state case has reached a final judgment? Later this term the Court also hears cases on assault-weapons bans, religious preschools and asylum status.

Why it matters

Dozens of similar state and city suits hang on this one. A broad ruling for the companies would effectively end climate liability litigation in state courts.

How each side sees it

Energy companies / federal government

Global emissions are an "inherently transboundary" problem. Letting "each State… tackle inherently national or international problems" through local juries would produce a patchwork of conflicting liability rules.

Via SCOTUSblog · Washington Legal Foundation (pro-business)
Boulder / climate litigants

This is a consumer-deception case, not emissions regulation. The companies "point to nothing in the text of the Constitution" that bars it, and the Court is stepping in before state courts have finished.

Via Just Security · Balls & Strikes (progressive)
Watch: whether Justices Gorsuch and Barrett, who sometimes side with states on federalism, are skeptical of the preemption argument.

OpenAI shelves a model over safety regressions as the fight over preempting state AI laws heats up

What happened

OpenAI reportedly scrapped the release of GPT-6.1 "Astra" after internal testing showed serious safety regressions. NewPontificate of Leo XIV

Ten years after Amoris Laetitia, Leo calls bishops to Rome to take stock

What happened

Pope Leo XIV is convening a closed-door meeting Oct 7–14 on how bishops have put Francis's 2016 exhortation into practice. That document opened a path for some divorced and civilly remarried Catholics to receive Communion. The Vatican stresses the text is "not up for rewriting, amending or scrapping." The meeting will use the synodal "Conversation in the Spirit" method and publish a public summary. Also this week: Leo returned from a four-day France trip (Sept 25–28). It included the first papal visit to the rebuilt Notre-Dame, a UNESCO speech on AI, and a private meeting with abuse survivors at Lourdes. His October prayer intention is mental-health ministry.

How each side sees it

Traditionalist view

The review is overdue. Critics see "Luther's shadow" in Amoris and want the meeting to restore clarity on marriage and the sacraments, not just record practice.

Via Return to Order (TFP) (traditionalist) · Crux
Francis-continuity view

This is a pastoral checkup on a settled teaching. Leo is listening to how accompaniment works locally, not reopening doctrine. The Vatican's own framing backs this up.

Via National Catholic Reporter (progressive) · Where Peter Is
Watch: the public summary report, and whether participants from Poland and Africa, where bishops' conferences resisted Amoris, speak publicly.

Blind spot

The Lukoil side deal is a big story in Kyiv and Moscow's exile press, and nearly absent on the American right

In our sweep, the report that Ukraine talks now include a Lukoil asset sale was covered by the NYT, The Hill, and Ukrainian and Russian-exile outlets (Kyiv Post, Ukrainska Pravda, Moscow Times, Liga). We found no coverage yet from major right-leaning US outlets. If you mostly read conservative media, you probably haven't seen this.

Their likely argument, which is also the negotiators' reported rationale: bringing Lukoil's global assets under Western ownership could lower energy prices and give Washington leverage over Moscow. Whether that's statecraft or a sweetheart deal is the debate worth following.
