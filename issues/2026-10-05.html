NewBrazil election · runoff Oct 25

Flávio Bolsonaro beats the polls and edges Lula in Brazil's first round

What happened

Since Sunday's issue: the official count shows Senator Flávio Bolsonaro with 47.03% of valid votes (about 56.1 million) and President Lula da Silva with 45.16% (about 53.9 million), according to Agência Brasil. Neither cleared 50%, so they meet in a runoff on Oct 25. The final polls had Lula ahead: Datafolha's Saturday survey had him at 45% to Flávio's 42%. Flávio's first-round total beat what his father, Jair Bolsonaro, won in 2022. Jair is in prison serving a 27-year sentence for the coup plot.

Why it matters

A sitting president trailing after round one goes into the runoff on the defensive. Polling also underestimated the right for the second election in a row; the same thing happened with Jair in 2022. The outcome decides whether Latin America's largest economy moves closer to Washington or keeps Lula's balance between the US and China.

How each side sees it

Bolsonaro camp

Flávio claimed a mandate for change, declaring "the end of the PT era." He had said before the vote that he was counting on protest votes from Brazilians more opposed to Lula than attached to him.

Via MercoPress · Latin America Daily Briefing
Lula camp

Speaking in São Paulo, Lula said "the second round is the real test" and that a new campaign starts immediately. His camp casts Flávio as a stand-in for a jailed father and a risk to institutions.

Via MercoPress · Al Jazeera
Watch: where the ~8% that went to other candidates (Caiado and others) goes, and the first runoff polls. Pollsters' credibility is now part of the story.

EscalatingIran war · day 220 (began Feb 28)

Iran's oil minister quits as the blockade chokes exports

What happened

Since Sunday's issue: President Pezeshkian accepted Oil Minister Mohsen Paknejad's resignation on Sunday, having turned down earlier requests to step down. Paknejad cited "personal reasons." Hamid Bovard, head of the National Iranian Oil Company, is acting minister. Iran's central bank chief says the US naval blockade has driven crude exports to zero. Fuel queues are reported at home, and the central bank is injecting up to $2B as the rial hits a record low. The IRGC-linked Fars agency named the tanker hit off Oman this weekend as the Ore Vinst and said it was under US escort. UKMTO counts it as the fourth attack it has reported this month. Haaretz, citing Axios, reports that Vance chaired a secret Camp David meeting on Iran and Yemen on Friday.

Why it matters

The resignation is the clearest sign yet that the blockade is hurting Iran's oil sector from the inside. But Tehran is answering with pressure on shipping, not concessions, and that keeps the Gulf risk premium in oil prices.

How each side sees it

Tehran's line

Parliament Speaker Qalibaf says attacks around Hormuz will continue until Iran's seven conditions are met. Officials call the minister's exit personal, and the foreign minister insists there is "no military solution."

Via Fox News live · The National (UAE)
Pressure-is-working view

Iran International asks what a new oil chief can change with exports at zero, and frames the resignation as the blockade's toll on a strained ministry. Trade press puts output near 1.8M barrels a day, with loadings collapsing since July.

Via Iran International (Persian diaspora, anti-regime) · Rigzone
Watch: whether Bovard is confirmed by parliament, and whether attacks reach the US-escorted lane again.

DevelopingEnergy crisis · Iran war day 220

Murban jumps to about $110 as traders pay up for barrels outside the Gulf risk zone

What happened

Monday's Asian session opened with Brent slightly higher after the weekend's Houthi claims. It later slipped to about $101, per Trading Economics, as traders weighed Sunday's OPEC+ decision to hold output and the G7 reserve release. Abu Dhabi's Murban traded near $110.80, about $8 over Brent, and the Brent–WTI spread is above $11, against a normal $3–5. The G7 release (up to 100M barrels over four months, about 830k barrels a day, diesel first) has no new delivery dates yet. Goldman estimates it offsets only about half the diesel price surge.

Why it matters

The spreads are the market's own risk map. Barrels that have to pass Hormuz or the Red Sea cost more, which hits Asian buyers first. The wide WTI discount also keeps US crude exports attractive, which shapes the US debate over diesel exports.

How each side sees it

"End the war, prices fall"

Trump says oil prices "will come tumbling down" once the war ends, and touts Europe's agreement to release "heavily stocked Diesel."

Via CBS News · Euronews
"It's a refining crisis"

Doomberg argues record diesel reflects a global shortage of refining capacity, not US exports. It warns that lawmakers pushing a diesel-export ban are grasping at straws.

Via Doomberg (energy realist) · Time (Goldman estimate)
Watch: today's EIA diesel print (the record is $6.529, from Sep 21) and whether Murban's premium narrows if Saudi exports hold.

NewSCOTUS · October Term 2026 opens today

The term opens with Big Oil vs. Boulder, without Alito

What happened

At 9 AM CT the justices hear Suncor Energy v. Boulder County. The question is whether federal law bars state-law claims for harm from interstate and international greenhouse-gas emissions. Boulder sued Suncor and ExxonMobil in 2018, and the Colorado Supreme Court let the case proceed in 2025. The Solicitor General is arguing alongside the companies. Kevin Russell argues for Boulder. Justice Alito recused himself without giving a reason, which leaves eight justices and the possibility of a 4–4 split that would let the Colorado ruling stand. Johnson v. U.S. Congress, on VA benefits for incarcerated veterans, follows.

Why it matters

Dozens of similar state and city climate suits depend on the answer. Previews single out Thomas and Gorsuch, who are often skeptical of broad federal preemption, as the votes to watch.

How each side sees it

Industry / right

A Daily Caller column calls the case "climate lawfare." It argues a Colorado court shouldn't set national energy policy through local damage awards.

Via Daily Caller (right) · The Center Square
Boulder / climate advocates

Supporters say this is an ordinary state consumer-deception case, not emissions regulation. The Union of Concerned Scientists called Alito's recusal the "bare minimum" given what it calls a conflict of interest.

Via Union of Concerned Scientists (climate advocacy, left) · Earthjustice
Watch: whether Thomas or Gorsuch presses the companies on federalism, and whether any justice raises the jurisdiction question, which could end the case without a merits ruling.

Trump names an AI czar with a "Super Intelligence Force," but no stated mission
