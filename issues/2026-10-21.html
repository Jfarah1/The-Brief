DevelopingUkraine war / peace track

Witkoff shuttles from Oslo to Putin; an American is freed, but no deal

What happened

Since Monday: on Tuesday in Oslo, US envoy Steve Witkoff met Zelensky at talks hosted by Norway's Støre and Finland's Stubb, with the UK, Germany, France, Poland, the EU and NATO taking part; Kushner, who is ill, was due to join remotely. On Wednesday Witkoff met Putin at the Constantine Palace in St. Petersburg and urged him to consider "resuming trilateral talks" with Ukraine and the US. Putin thanked Trump and announced the release of US citizen Stephen Hubbard (Kyiv Independent). Russian state agency TASS presents the release as goodwill; Hubbard was serving 6 years 10 months for fighting for Ukraine. TASS says a Putin–Trump meeting in China next month was discussed. Trump said of a three-way meeting, "They don't have to be in the same room." The Kremlin still rejects a ceasefire and demands that Kyiv give up the rest of Donbas. Earlier, Rubio said US air-defense delays were not deliberate. Poland's Sikorski said Poland has no Patriot missiles left to give. Kyiv says Washington is using aid, Starlink limits and intelligence to push it to stop hitting Russian refineries. Russian strikes killed at least 16 and injured 69 in the day to Tuesday, hitting a Kyiv hospital and power plants, with outages in about half of Ukraine's regions. At least 9 more were killed by Wednesday morning, 2 of them in Kyiv. Ukraine hit Yandex data centers and energy sites in occupied Donetsk (Kyiv Independent). Hungary blocked the next step of EU accession for Ukraine and Moldova.

Why it matters

Washington is talking to both capitals within 24 hours, but the outline Moscow wants, a Trump–Putin deal in China without Kyiv, is still the one on the table. Meanwhile Ukraine's grid is being hit as winter begins.

How each side sees it

Via TASS (Russian state media) · Moscow Times (Russian exile; headline only)

Via ISW, via Kyiv Post (hawkish think tank; Oct 20)

EscalatingIran war · day 236 (began Feb 28)

Tehran hardens its line as mine claims and new hits roil Hormuz

What happened

Since Monday: Russia's Lavrov met Araghchi in Tehran on Monday, his first visit since the war began, saying he aims to prevent "the return of a large-scale war." On Tuesday Araghchi said Iran and Russia have never worked so closely (CBS). Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir flew to Tehran Tuesday with proposals, and Qatar says US–Iran contact has "not stopped." Tehran's response was cold. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei rejected the Pakistani proposals and said no enriched material will leave Iran. Speaker Ghalibaf told Munir Iran had "nothing new to say," and President Pezeshkian ruled out talks under US pressure (Gulf News). In an interview aired Wednesday, Araghchi said the strait "will not be open to everyone" until a deal is reached. In an FT op-ed he wrote there is "little reason to expect progress now" (The National). Iranian officials told Reuters they see little chance of a breakthrough before the US election. Iranian officials also threatened undersea cables and data centers if fighting resumes. At sea, the IRGC says three tankers on a southern route near Oman that it calls "illegal" hit mines Tuesday: two caught fire and a third, carrying more than 2 million barrels, exploded. None of this is independently verified (Arab News). Iran's Tasnim, an IRGC-linked state outlet, claims a 54th US drone downed (headline only). UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an "unknown projectile" off Bukha, Oman, this morning; the crew is safe. The IMO's secretary-general said his agency can't guarantee ships will avoid mines (Fox). On the US side, Trump told a Kansas rally Iran "won't" get a nuclear weapon. Defense Secretary Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Israel Monday. Fox reports the USS Theodore Roosevelt group is heading to the region, which would make three carriers there.

Why it matters

Last week's seven-day plan has turned into a flat refusal to talk on US terms before the midterms. Mine claims on the Oman-side route, the main workaround, are what pushed Brent to a five-month high on Tuesday.

How each side sees it

Tehran: the strait is our leverage

Araghchi calls the nuclear issue "nothing more than a pretext" and says Hormuz stays closed until there is an understanding. Rezaei sets seven conditions before any talks.

Via The National (UAE) · Gulf News (UAE)
Washington: pressure until Iran yields

Trump framed the choice for Tehran as winning "the nice way or the not nice way," while keeping his no-strikes-before-the-vote pledge. CENTCOM calls the IRGC's mine claims false.

Via CBS News · Arab News (Saudi, government-aligned)
Shipping: the route isn't safe

IMO chief Arsenio Dominguez says the strait is "not open for business in the way that it was before," and that the 1968 traffic separation scheme can't be called safe.

Via Fox News (right)

DevelopingUS–China tech

China's AI export controls take shape as its giants scramble for Nvidia cards

What happened

Since Friday: details of Beijing's Oct 16 draft (MOFCOM/MOST No. 48) are clearer. It adds 40 technologies to the export-control catalog, 23 of them in AI, with 14 banned outright and 26 requiring licenses. Models are covered at 2 trillion or more parameters, 10^26 or more training FLOPs, or a global top-10 ranking, including weights and source code. Open-source and published research are exempt. Comments are due Nov 15 (Business Standard, SCMP). Separately, Reuters, via the Taipei Times, reports that Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance ordered more than 3 million export-compliant RTX PRO 5500 GPUs, worth $35B or more, though Nvidia has reportedly told partners they may get only 5–10% of their orders. Neither company has confirmed the orders on the record.

How each side sees it

Chinese analysts: short-term pain, long-term gain

iiMedia's Zhang Yi says the curbs bring short-term adaptation costs but protect China's lead technologies; Omdia's Lian Jye Su calls them a mirror of Western AI-sovereignty rules.

Via SCMP (Hong Kong, Alibaba-owned; snippet)
Western hawks: a hit to China's open models abroad

ASPI's Alex Colville says the rules could delay or block Chinese open-weight models with global cloud providers. Former US trade official Wendy Cutler expects a US response.

Via SCMP · The National (UAE; Oct 16)
Watch: US Commerce response; Nov 15 comment deadline; Nvidia's China allocations.

Back in St. Peter's Square, Leo makes the family his first post-surgery theme

What happened

Since Monday: Pope Leo held his first general audience since his lung-cancer surgery this morning in St. Peter's Square and thanked the faithful for their prayers. It was the 29th catechesis in his Vatican II series and covered Gaudium et Spes 47–52 on marriage and family. He said supporting families is "more urgent than ever before," affirmed the "unbreakable oneness" of spouses, and condemned abortion and infanticide (official text). On Monday he phoned Zelensky to urge humanitarian aid and named new members of the International Theological Commission (Vatican News). His surgeon has said there's no clinical reason to cancel the Nov 6–17 South America trip, and The Pillar is previewing the Uruguay leg. The cancer's stage is still undisclosed.

How each side sees it

Conservatives: clear restatement of doctrine

CatholicCulture.org and EWTN lead with Leo upholding indissolubility and teaching on contraception, including that spouses' consciences must follow the Magisterium.

Via CatholicCulture.org (conservative) · EWTN News (conservative)
Pro-Francis: pastoral return

Where Peter Is leads with Leo's thanks and his call for support for families. The same roundup links a piece asking whether a decade of Amoris Laetitia debate focused on the wrong question.

Via Where Peter Is (pro-Francis/Leo) · Aleteia (Catholic, center)
Watch: Pathology disclosure; final trip schedule; the Amoris review.

NewUS Church

Denver tells celebrity exorcist Fr. Chad Ripperger to go silent for a year

What happened

The Archdiocese of Denver said Monday that Archbishop James Golka asked Fr. Chad Ripperger, a widely followed exorcist and podcast guest, to take a one-year "fast" from public speaking and interviews, and that he agreed. No reason was given and no misconduct finding was announced (OSV News, via NCR). LifeSiteNews, as reported by the Daily Caller, says the restriction also covers online homilies and writings until next September. Where Peter Is reports, from correspondence it obtained, that Denver had already restricted him in 2025 after reviewing his statements on mental health. The archdiocese hasn't said whether the two are linked.

How each side sees it

Progressives: overdue oversight

NCR and Where Peter Is frame it as a bishop reining in a priest whose claims about demons and mental health had drawn concern.

Via National Catholic Reporter / OSV News (progressive / Catholic wire) · Where Peter Is
Traditionalists: silencing, not a "fast"

Big Modernism mocks the voluntary framing as "another year of synodal listening, provided nobody speaks." The Daily Caller emphasizes his big audiences with Rogan and Carlson.

Via Big Modernism (traditionalist Substack) · Daily Caller (right) · EWTN
Watch: Whether Denver explains the decision; any reaction from Ripperger's order or followers.
