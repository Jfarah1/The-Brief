EscalatingIran war · day 262 (began Feb 28)

A third US carrier arrives as Tehran says it "will not wait" to be hit first

What happened

Since Friday: CENTCOM said US forces shot down two Revolutionary Guards drones headed for Bahrain Friday, with "no injuries or damage," and rejected the IRGC's claim that it destroyed an early-warning radar. The IRGC called it retaliation for the strike on a Sirik telecom tower (Iran International). Saturday, Reuters reported that Trump may hold off on new strikes unless Iran or the Houthis provoke him, after Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Turkish President Erdogan urged restraint; the blockade of Iranian ports continues (via Times of Israel). Sunday the USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in CENTCOM's area, joining the George H.W. Bush and George Washington. That's three carriers in the region for the first time since April, though the Pentagon hasn't formally confirmed it. Bahrain said Sunday its air defenses intercepted several Iranian attacks, and the IRGC claimed it hit a US radar with six drones; that hasn't been confirmed. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the IAEA will get no access until "the war has permanently ended" (Times of Israel). The IRGC says six tankers caught fire after hitting mines Sunday and Monday. Reuters couldn't verify that, and CENTCOM says the main routes are clear. Separately, the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX was hit by an "unknown projectile" off Oman, and all crew are safe (Iran International live blog). Foreign Minister Araghchi said in Islamabad Monday that "Pakistan is doing its job" as a mediator. He goes to Oman Tuesday.

Why it matters

Washington is pairing a pause on new strikes with the largest naval buildup in months. Whether Hormuz incidents or a Houthi strike count as the "provocation" that ends the pause will decide whether the war resumes in full.

How each side sees it

Tehran: we won't absorb the first blow

Gharibabadi says Iran "will not wait for the US to attack first" and accuses Washington of abusing diplomacy. An MP says Iran will keep enriching to 60% and won't dilute its roughly 400 kg stockpile.

Via Times of Israel (Israeli, center) · Iran International (diaspora, anti-regime)
Washington: pressure is doing the work

Rubio says the pressure is so damaging "there is no need for major combat operations." Treasury Secretary Bessent: "this is one of the fastest collapses in history," forecasting a roughly 25% drop in Iran's GDP.

Via Iran International (diaspora, anti-regime)
Analysts: deterrence that can misfire

Crisis Group's Ali Vaez warns the buildup could "ignite one that neither can win, contain or easily end." Haaretz's Amos Harel writes that Netanyahu, though boxed in from several directions, is still pushing for another strike.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded) · Haaretz (Israeli, left; headline only, paywalled)
Watch: Araghchi in Oman Tuesday; Pentagon confirmation of the three-carrier posture; more mine incidents; Iran on the Shenzhen agenda Thursday; Democrats' Dec 11 funding lever (no new text from Kaine this weekend).

NewAPEC / US–China–Russia

Trump, Xi and Putin set their first three-way summit, with no deal expected

What happened

On Friday the White House said Trump, Xi and Putin will meet together Thursday Nov 19 in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of APEC. The Kremlin and China's Foreign Ministry confirmed it over the weekend (TASS, Russian state media; Al Jazeera). China's Mao Ning called the meeting of "groundbreaking significance." US officials say the agenda is nuclear arms control and the Iran war. A senior official said "we do not expect to get any kind of agreement." The White House says Ukraine isn't on the agenda and there will be no separate Trump–Putin meeting (HNGN). On arms control, China's Lin Jian said Beijing "will not participate in disarmament negotiations at this stage." New START lapsed in February, and China has about 620 warheads by SIPRI's estimate. Trump leaves today, with stops in Manila Tuesday for the Quad and a meeting with Southeast Asian leaders, and a meeting with Marcos Wednesday. In the background, Bessent said Trump made clear China "should not receive any Nvidia chips above H100s," and analysts quoted by SCMP say Beijing could tighten rare-earth exports if chip curbs escalate.

Why it matters

It's the first time the three nuclear powers' leaders sit at one table. With New START gone and Iran at war, low expectations still leave room for an incident-prevention or communication channel.

How each side sees it

Beijing: a model for great-power coexistence

A Global Times editorial calls the summit "a new path for the peaceful coexistence of great powers."

Via Global Times (Chinese state media) · TASS (Russian state media)
Skeptics: no trust, and a gift to Putin

HKU's Alejandro Reyes: "The fundamental obstacle is political trust." Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, says a US–China–Russia meeting would "only encourage Putin to continue this war" (as relayed in a wire roundup).

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded) · SCMP (Hong Kong)
Watch: Whether a separate Trump–Xi meeting is confirmed (ABC reports a bilateral and working lunch); any arms-control or AI-incident channel; chip-export language.

DevelopingUkraine war / peace track

Zelensky says the US floated Donbas for NATO; allies set Berlin for Thursday

What happened

Since Friday: Zelensky said US negotiators asked whether Ukraine would leave the Donbas in exchange for NATO membership. He called it a question rather than an offer, and said it was the first time the two had been linked. Kyiv isn't ready to give up territory, he said, but a full package of guarantees, a strong army and rebuilding money could go to a referendum (RTÉ; Kyiv Post). He also said the US ruled out sending troops and described bilateral guarantees modeled on Israel's (Kyiv Independent). Germany confirmed the Coalition of the Willing will meet in Berlin Thursday Nov 19, co-chaired with the UK and France; Chancellor Merz expects more than 20 leaders (Interfax-Ukraine). The prisoner swap of more than 1,000 per side still hasn't happened, and there's no date for the next round of talks. Overnight into Sunday, Russia launched 132 drones; strikes on energy sites left about 41,000 families in Odesa Oblast without power, and at least 4 people were killed nationwide that day. Ukraine's General Staff reported strikes on Akhtubinsk airfield, a Tatarstan refinery and petrochemical plant, and an aircraft plant in Bryansk Oblast (Kyiv Independent).

Why it matters

Linking territory to NATO membership would be a new kind of trade, and the US hasn't confirmed it put the question. Europe's Berlin meeting falls the same day Trump sits down with Putin and Xi in Shenzhen.

Why it matters

DevelopingFed / inflation

Wholesale inflation hits a four-year high, and Treasury tells the Fed not to hike

What happened

Since Friday: October producer prices rose 0.3%, a bit below the 0.4% expected, but they're up 6.5% from a year ago, the most since November 2022, and September was revised up to 1.4%. Prices excluding food, energy and trade rose 0.5% on the month (BLS). Excluding just food and energy, they rose 0.3%, in line with forecasts, and 5% on the year (CNN). Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called inflation "borderline disturbing" and said the Fed isn't on track back to 2% (Fortune). Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said he takes his cues from where inflation is heading (Reuters via Investing.com). CME FedWatch shows a December 9 hike fully priced, with roughly 11–14% odds of a half-point. Treasury Secretary Bessent pushed back in public, warning against hikes during an energy supply shock (Al Jazeera). The 10-year yield ended the week near 5.14%.

Why it matters

The Treasury is now openly lobbying against a rate hike that markets treat as certain, which puts the Fed's independence on the line along with its inflation record.

How each side sees it

Hawks: credibility is at stake

Goolsbee: "We've been above 2% for almost six years." KPMG's Diane Swonk says the "Fed is losing its inflation fighting credibility"; FWDBONDS's Chris Rupkey saw "no silver lining" in the PPI.

Via Fortune (business magazine) · CNBC (mainstream)

Bessent: "The Fed's supposed to look into the future, not look in the rearview mirror," arguing energy-driven inflation will fade as supply recovers.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded)

DevelopingEnergy / diesel crisis

Oil flat to close a losing week, caught between an IEA glut and burning tankers

What happened

Since Friday: Brent settled up 2 cents at $95.84 Friday and WTI down 18 cents at $88.56, both off about 2.5% on the week (EnergyNow; Xinhua, Chinese state media). The IEA's Friday report sees a fourth-quarter surplus of about 1.7 million barrels a day and roughly 3.8 million in 2027, but calls diesel tightness "the overriding concern." US distillate stocks are about 103 million barrels, against 111 million a year ago (Energy News Beat). Over the weekend, Iran's mine and tanker-fire claims, the Bahrain attacks and the Houthi missiles at Riyadh pushed prices both ways. Brent was near $95.15 early Monday, then about $96.13 by 6:20 AM CT (CNBC). Goldman's 2027 outlook has Brent averaging $88 and falling to $78 by the end of 2027. OPEC+ meets Nov 29 to set 2027 quotas. Ukraine's strikes on the Volgograd refinery and Tatarstan's TAIF-NK keep pressure on Russian product exports.

Why it matters

Crude may be loosening, but diesel isn't. Retail diesel is still above $6, and truckers and farmers feel that first.

How each side sees it

NewSCOTUS October Term 2026

The Supreme Court will decide whether Trump can suspend asylum at the border

What happened

Friday the Court agreed to hear Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center (RAICES), the administration's appeal over its suspension of asylum at the southern border. It will also decide whether Judge Randolph Moss could grant class-wide relief, and it declined a separate class-certification question. Argument is likely next spring. The Court also took Robinhood v. Sodha, a securities case (SCOTUSblog). SCOTUSblog calls the term "short on cases, period." There are no arguments this week; the next are Nov 30. Still pending with no action found: the White House ballroom application, and Judge Christopher Cooper's ruling on a class-wide injunction in the second birthright-citizenship case. In Washington state, a temporary order bars DOJ from forcing officials to set aside about 400 ballots it alleges were cast by noncitizens; the injunction hearing is Wednesday (Washington State Standard). Today's orders list is due at 8:30 AM CT.

Why it matters

The case tests whether a president can shut off a statutory right to seek asylum by proclamation. The class-relief question could also limit how lower courts block policies nationwide.

How each side sees it

Administration: emergency power at the border

The administration is appealing Judge Moss's ruling against the suspension and his class-wide relief, defending the policy as within the president's power over entry at the border.

Via SCOTUSblog (nonpartisan)
Challengers: the statute guarantees a hearing

RAICES and the other plaintiffs, who won in the lower courts, contend the asylum statute can't be suspended by proclamation.

Via SCOTUSblog (nonpartisan)
