DevelopingIran war · day 257 (began Feb 28)

Trump calls the war "effectively over" but keeps the blockade; tankers are hit around Hormuz

What happened

Since Monday: after meeting Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani at the White House, Trump said the war is "militarily, effectively over" and new strikes are "quite unlikely," but that the US will keep the "status quo": the naval blockade plus a new operation, Iron Gate, aimed at Iran's land and Caspian trade (Al Jazeera). He said Gulf states "would prefer the status quo." Qatar's foreign ministry said Tuesday its mediation had "gained momentum" (Iran International). Reports citing Turkey's intelligence chief said Qatar would host Vance and Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei next week; Foreign Minister Araghchi denied any meeting is planned, and Tasnim said there will be no talks until the US takes concrete steps on Iran's seven conditions (Euronews). Iran said Qeshm was struck again overnight into Tuesday; the US hasn't acknowledged it. On Tuesday the IRGC said three tankers were on fire in the strait, ADNOC said one of its ships was damaged in an attack in UAE waters, and UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an "unknown projectile" off Oman (Dawn). Early Wednesday, Al Arabiya reported a tanker on fire after hitting a mine, citing Iran's army; we couldn't open the article. Rubio says the economic campaign means "there is no need for major combat operations."

Why it matters

"Over" now means a siege rather than a settlement. Oil markets are treating it as peace, but ships are still being hit, and Iran's conditions remain far from Washington's.

How each side sees it

Tehran: no surrender, no talks yet

Rezaei told Washington to stop "manufacturing FAKE NEWS about imaginary talks"; President Pezeshkian: "We will not surrender to force and bullying." Araghchi says the war won't be resolved by force "nor by intensifying sanctions," and the two-day plan stays on the table.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded) · Dawn (Pakistani, center)
Washington: squeeze, not bomb

Rubio: "The economic pain that is being inflicted on Iran right now is extraordinary, and it compounds." Hegseth says forces remain "locked and loaded."

Via Iran International (diaspora, anti-regime) · Euronews (European)
Skeptics: a siege sold as peace

National Security Journal's Steve Balestrieri: "Wars don't end because one side says so… It has settled into a siege." (The page carries no date; its content refers to Monday's remarks.)

DevelopingUkraine war / peace track

Abu Dhabi produces a big prisoner swap and a trip to Moscow, but no energy truce

What happened

Since Monday: the US, Russia and Ukraine met in Abu Dhabi Tuesday and Wednesday, their first three-way talks since February. Day one ran about four and a half hours; Kushner set four areas: ending hostilities, post-war security guarantees, economic recovery and humanitarian issues (Kyiv Post). European, UK and NATO officials came to Abu Dhabi but stayed outside the room (Meduza). Zelensky called the talks "constructive and quite substantive" and said a swap could be 1,000-for-1,000 or 1,500-for-1,500; he said Moscow claimed to hold half of Donbas, which Kyiv disputes (Kyiv Independent). On Wednesday Kushner said the sides agreed "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners," with numbers due Thursday, and that he and Witkoff will meet Putin in Moscow Thursday, then brief Zelensky in Dublin (France 24). No energy or maritime truce was announced. The bombing continued: Russia launched 317 drones and cruise missiles overnight into Monday, damaging two energy sites in Kyiv region and injuring 28 people nationwide, and overnight into Wednesday it hit six substations, rail lines and a market in Chornomorsk, killing 3. Ukrainian drones set fire to Russia's Kirishi refinery on Sunday night (Kyiv Independent; France 24).

Why it matters

A prisoner deal and a Putin meeting are the most concrete movement in nine months, but neither side has shifted on territory, and the strikes on power and refineries are continuing as winter sets in.

How each side sees it

Kyiv: momentum, prisoners first

Zelensky: "The release of prisoners is a priority," and the sides discussed "the need for de-escalation steps." Negotiator Arakhamia sees "good momentum" toward a deal before year-end.

Via Kyiv Independent (Ukrainian) · Interfax-Ukraine (Ukrainian agency)
Moscow: nothing has changed

Lavrov: "Our position has not changed." Peskov says details stay "behind closed doors" and no contacts with Europeans were planned.

Via TASS (Russian state media) · Meduza (exile, anti-Kremlin)
Washington: openings

Kushner: "we see some openings." Rubio earlier: "Nothing's been finalized yet."

Via France 24 (French public)
Watch: Final swap numbers Thursday; what Putin tells Kushner and Witkoff; Russia's refinery losses (OilPrice.com puts disruption near 30% of capacity).

EscalatingSaudi–Houthi war · since July

Houthis hit the East-West pipeline and keep firing at Riyadh; schools go online

What happened

Since Monday: on Monday a Houthi attack hit Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline in the Medina region; a fire was put out, no one was hurt, and the Energy Ministry said "pipeline operations were unaffected" (Al Jazeera). On Tuesday coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said a ballistic missile and a cruise missile were downed over Riyadh and another ballistic missile over the south, plus a drone over the Eastern Province. Riyadh residents got shelter alerts for a third straight day, and the capital's schools moved online with no end date (Middle East Eye). Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed hits on King Khalid airport, pipeline pumping stations, Riyadh's financial district and Abqaiq, calling it retaliation for a Saudi strike on a market in Taiz; none is verified. Houthi-run Saba reported Saudi strikes on a gas facility and an oil company building in Sanaa, which the coalition hasn't confirmed; the Houthi health ministry now says weekend strikes killed at least 44, including 21 children, an unverified figure up from 33.

Why it matters

The East-West pipeline is Saudi Arabia's route around Hormuz. Repeated hits on it, and on Riyadh, are the main Gulf supply risk the oil market isn't pricing right now.

How each side sees it

Riyadh: defenses are holding

Saudi officials stress interceptions and say the pipeline kept running.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded)
Houthis: retaliation, and Saudi airspace "not safe"

The Houthis frame their strikes as answers to Saudi bombing of Taiz and Sanaa and warn Saudi airspace is "not safe."

Via Middle East Eye (UK-based, Arab-world, left)

October inflation runs hot, a December hike is fully priced, and Waller floats a half-point

What happened

Since Monday: consumer prices rose 0.4% in October against a 0.3% forecast, after 0.6% in September. Core prices rose 0.3% against 0.2% expected, the third straight 0.3% month. Energy rose 0.7%, food 0.5% and shelter 0.3%; used cars fell 1.2%. There's no year-on-year figure because October 2025 prices weren't collected during that year's shutdown. James Lankford said "Stephen is smart and tough" (The Bulwark). Government funding runs out Dec 11, and a stopgap into early next year is widely expected.

DevelopingCOP31 · Antalya, Nov 9–20

COP31 launches a "35x35" electrification pledge to few signatures; about 40 leaders arrive today

What happened

Since Monday: the Turkish presidency launched its "35x35" pledge on Tuesday, aiming to raise electricity's share of final energy use from about 23% to 35% by 2035, with an IEA tracking mechanism. Only Australia, Ethiopia, Germany and Norway have signed publicly; Climate Action Tracker projects current policies reach 28%. Brazil's COP30 presidency presented a roadmap for transitioning away from fossil fuels, and Guterres said it is "long past time to stop treating the transition away from fossil fuels as taboo." The same day, President Erdoğan hailed new oil output at Turkey's Gabar field. Transport chaos at a venue about 25 km from the city forced events to be rescheduled (Climate Home News). Reporter Ed King's sources say Brazil, Korea and Canada also signed, and that the EU hesitates because the text doesn't say "clean" power (Climate Diplomacy Brief). The leaders' summit opens today with about 40 leaders after Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, the Netherlands and Sweden dropped out. Guterres, at his last COP as UN chief, is proposing a "climate justice compact."

Why it matters

The summit's signature deliverable is struggling for signatures, and the host is expanding oil output. Credibility, not new targets, is what's being tested in Antalya.

How each side sees it

Host and UN: urgency and finance

Erdoğan says "humanity is going through one of the worst existential crises" and wants finance that is "more accessible, predictable"; Guterres: "polluters must pay."

Via Daily Sabah (Turkish, pro-government) · Climate Home News (climate diplomacy)
Critics: a pledge without "clean"

EU hesitancy reflects concern that the pledge counts any electricity, including fossil-fired power. Ed King also asks, half-seriously, whether the White House is "the best thing for climate since Covid," and concludes probably not overall.

Via Climate Diplomacy Brief (independent newsletter) · Climate Home News
