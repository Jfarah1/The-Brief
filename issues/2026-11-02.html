EscalatingIran war · day 248 (began Feb 28)

Tehran praises Vance's tone but holds its conditions as negotiators go to Doha and strike talk focuses on Wednesday

What happened

Since Friday: Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said on state TV late Saturday, "I have studied the letter, but I have not yet answered it." He said Washington's tone "has completely changed, and this is a good sign," but repeated that Iran won't reopen the Strait of Hormuz or resume talks until the US meets its seven conditions, which include ending hostilities, lifting port restrictions and releasing frozen assets (The National; Xinhua, state media). Foreign Minister Araghchi called the outreach "a positive step" but said there are "no plans currently" to meet US officials. Qatari mediators spent more than 12 hours in Tehran on Friday, and Iranian negotiators were in Doha on Sunday, according to a source cited by The National. At the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta on Saturday, Secretary of State Rubio said Trump had set no deadline and made no decision on new strikes: "If there is a diplomatic solution, we would be open to it." Three Gulf officials told MSNOW that strikes could come as early as Nov 4, and Trump told Netanyahu by phone Saturday that he was considering resuming them (Latin Times). Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered an "immediate, impactful, and geographically unrestricted response" to any threat. Separately, Hegseth removed Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia as Joint Staff J5 after Axios reported he admitted leaking Iran and Ukraine information; the case went to DOJ (via Gulf News).

Why it matters

Both sides now say diplomacy is possible while preparing for force, and the reported strike window opens the day after the US vote. Iran's answer, or its silence, before Wednesday is the main marker.

How each side sees it

Tehran: a better tone, the same terms

Rezaei and Araghchi welcome the change in tone but tie any talks to the seven conditions first. Iran's government-aligned press stresses that neither side has published the letter, so its contents rest on anonymous officials.

Via Xinhua (Chinese state media) · Tehran Times (Iranian state-aligned)
Washington: open to a deal, ready if not

Rubio says any agreement must address core US security concerns and that the US can act militarily if diplomacy fails. Trump says the US could destroy Iran's remaining nuclear sites "in ten minutes."

Via The National (UAE) · Latin Times (US digital, citing MSNOW)
⚠ We didn't find a US right-leaning outlet's argument for or against strikes this cycle.
Mediators and analysts: two tracks at once

Hamidreza Azizi of the International Crisis Group says "diplomacy and military preparations are proceeding simultaneously." Pakistan's military spokesman is "cautiously optimistic" but warns "powerful spoilers" could sink the talks.

Via The National · Gulf News (UAE)
Watch: A formal Iranian reply; whether the Doha contacts produce a talks date; US force movements after Tuesday.

DevelopingIsraeli election · voted Oct 27

No majority, and Eisenkot rules out serving under Netanyahu

What happened

Since Friday: no change in the count. Netanyahu's bloc has 53 seats (Likud 27, ultra-Orthodox parties 13), Eisenkot's bloc 48 (Yashar 21, Bennett's Together 12, the Democrats 8, Yisrael Beiteinu 7), and the Arab lists 19 (Joint List 11, Raam 8), per AFP via Arab News. Netanyahu's camp has floated a unity government, including a possible rotation, which Eisenkot rejected: "There will be only one prime minister, and it will be me." Lieberman says the only conceivable coalition is one without Netanyahu, and he still refuses to sit with ultra-Orthodox or Arab parties. Ynet reports Eisenkot is expected to get the first mandate. President Herzog says a new election "must be avoided at all costs." Official results are published Wednesday.

Why it matters

Lieberman's veto on the Arab and ultra-Orthodox parties closes the obvious routes to 61 for both camps, so the realistic options are a unity deal, a minority government backed from outside, or yet another election.

How each side sees it

The opposition: a mandate for change

Eisenkot says voters "have chosen change" and that he'll form "a new government that is good for Israel," not join Netanyahu's.

Via Arab News (Saudi, government-aligned; AFP copy) · Ynet (Israeli, center)
Netanyahu's camp: keep the door open

Netanyahu has kept the unity question open, and his allies point to Likud as the largest party. The ultra-Orthodox parties demand a military-service exemption law as their price.

Via Ynet · Organiser (Indian, Hindu-nationalist, pro-Netanyahu)
Watch: Official results Wednesday; whom Herzog names; any crack in Lieberman's veto.

DevelopingUkraine war / peace track

Families reunite as Oleshky's six-day truce reaches its end

What happened

Since Friday: hundreds of civilians left Russian-occupied Oleshky under the local ceasefire, traveling by bus across Crimea to Anapa, flying to Belarus and then crossing into Ukraine (Al Jazeera; Vatican News). Officials haven't said how many left or how many stayed; the town had shrunk from about 24,000 before the war to about 1,700, with no gas, power or running water, and food deliveries reportedly stopped in May. Russia's ombudsman Yana Lantratova said "the mission has come to an end." Accounts differ on when the truce expires: Al Jazeera said Sunday evening, Vatican News "by Monday." We found no report confirming whether it lapsed or was extended. Zelensky said "further steps toward de-escalation" are needed. Fighting and drone strikes went on elsewhere along the front.

Why it matters

The six days are the longest local pause in fighting since 2022, credited to Melania Trump with help from Pope Leo. Whether it becomes a template depends on talks reported for later in November.

How each side sees it

Kyiv: a first step, not a peace

Zelensky welcomed the evacuation but called for wider de-escalation, as Russian strikes continued elsewhere.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded; AP/Reuters copy)
Washington and the Vatican: proof that mediation works

The White House credits the First Lady, and Trump says "she does better than our professionals." Pope Leo hoped the cooperation would "lead to new concrete peace initiatives."

Via Vatican News (official) · White House (primary)

EscalatingEnergy / diesel crisis

First successful limpet-mine attack in Hormuz lifts WTI to a seven-week high; OPEC+ stands pat

What happened

Since Friday: UKMTO confirmed a tanker was hit in the strait on Thursday. The FT identified it as the Louise 1, and Euronews said it appears to be the first successful limpet-mine attack since the war began. The fire was put out, no one was hurt and the ship continued. UKMTO didn't attribute the attack. The IRGC claimed several tankers on "unauthorized" routes hit mines; its count varied between two, three and four across outlets, and no maritime authority has confirmed that claim (Straits Daily Brief). Oman evacuated 25 crew from a Panama-flagged vessel off Musandam. CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper said the main shipping lanes had been cleared of Iranian mines. WTI settled up 2.1% at $104.12 Friday, its highest close since Sept 15 and a fourth straight monthly gain. The seven-country OPEC+ group met virtually Sunday and kept December output at September's required levels; it meets next on Nov 29 (OPEC). Its targets remain above what members can ship through a blockaded Gulf.

Why it matters

Mines attached to hulls are harder to sweep than moored ones, so cleared lanes may not mean safe passage. Any US strike this week would test both.

How each side sees it

Tehran: its rules govern the strait

The IRGC says the stricken tankers were on routes it hadn't authorized and were "brought to a halt" south of the strait.

Via Euronews (European) · Gulf News (UAE)
Washington and monitors: lanes are open, claims unverified

CENTCOM says the main lanes are clear and denies an IRGC claim to have seized a US drone; maritime monitors haven't confirmed Iran's multi-tanker claim.

Via Straits Daily Brief (maritime tracker) · Jerusalem Post (Israeli, center-right)
Producers: no change until capacity is known

OPEC+ kept quotas flat and is waiting on a capacity review; delegates have said changes are unlikely before 2027.

Via OPEC (primary) · Enerdata (energy trade)
Watch: EIA diesel this afternoon (record is $6.53); tanker insurance rates; any Hormuz incident after Tuesday.

DevelopingVoting-law / executive-power rulings

DOJ loses in Alaska and again in the Ninth Circuit days before the vote

What happened

Since Friday: on Sunday morning U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed DOJ's suit to stop Alaska from certifying its Senate race unless incumbent Republican Dan S. Sullivan wins, in a 20-page decision (Alaska Public Media). DOJ had asked that ballots marked for Daniel J. Sullivan, a retired Petersburg teacher on the ballot with no party label whom Republicans call a Democratic plant, be set aside. Gleason found DOJ lacked standing, offered "no workable plan" to separate ballots while keeping them secret, and that segregation could confuse voters and keep them home; she refused leave to refile. DOJ filed a notice of appeal within an hour. Separate federal and state investigations into alleged candidate recruitment continue; Peltola denies involvement. On Friday a Ninth Circuit panel ruled 2–1 (Bennett and Sanchez; Bumatay dissenting) not to stop California from counting about 20,000 ballots DOJ links to "likely noncitizens." The majority said there is "no feasible way" to segregate specific voters' ballots and the request likely violates the National Voter Registration Act. DOJ is expected to go to the Supreme Court; we found no filing yet.

Why it matters

Courts have now refused at every level so far to let the federal government hold back ballots before they're counted. That leaves post-election challenges and the Supreme Court as the remaining routes, in races that could decide the Senate.

How each side sees it

Voting-rights side: an attempt to subvert the count

Democracy Docket calls the rulings a major win for voters and a blow to "escalating efforts to subvert the midterms." Alaska's elections division called DOJ's plan "half-baked."

Via Democracy Docket (left, voting-rights) · Alaska Public Media (public radio, nonpartisan)
Administration side: a modest remedy against diluted votes

Judge Bumatay's dissent calls DOJ's request "a modest remedy" against noncitizen ballots "threatening to dilute the vote of eligible citizens." Trump and Sullivan call the second Sullivan a "sham," and conservative outlets have pressed the noncitizen-ballot case.

Via California Globe (right) · Ninth Circuit dissent (primary, quoted by Democracy Docket)
Watch: A DOJ emergency application to the Supreme Court; the Ninth Circuit's handling of the Alaska appeal; the DHS ads hearing Wednesday.

NewSCOTUS October Term 2026 · November sitting

On Election Day, the justices weigh whether to overrule a landmark religion precedent

What happened

The Court's November sitting opens today. On Tuesday it hears St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy (No. 25-581): two Denver-area parishes, two Catholic parents and the Archdiocese of Denver challenge their exclusion from Colorado's universal preschool program because they won't commit to admit children of LGBTQ parents on equal terms. The Tenth Circuit upheld the rule as neutral and generally applicable under Employment Division v. Smith (1990), and the petitioners ask the Court to overrule Smith. The Trump administration and 21 Republican-led states back the parishes; 18 Democratic-led states back Colorado (National Constitution Center; Supreme Court calendar). Other cases this sitting include Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission and Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards (Federalist Society).

Why it matters

Smith sets the default test for nearly every free-exercise claim. Overruling it would change how courts weigh religious objections to anti-discrimination, health and school laws.

How each side sees it

The parishes: religious families shut out

Supporters argue a "universal" program can't exclude Catholic schools, and families, for following church teaching, and that Smith leaves believers unprotected.

Via The Daily Signal (Heritage, right) · The Federalist (right)
Colorado's side: public money, equal access

Colorado says its conditions apply to every provider. LGBTQ advocates and interfaith groups argue that publicly funded schools shouldn't turn children away because of who their parents are.

Via Human Rights Campaign (LGBTQ advocacy, left) · Interfaith Alliance (progressive, religious-liberty)
Watch: Whether Justices Barrett and Kavanaugh, who questioned replacing Smith in 2021, signal a willingness to do it now.

NewLeo's pontificate · Hallowtide

The South's first beatification, an All Saints' call to ordinary holiness, and All Souls' at Verano

What happened

Since Friday: on Saturday in Savannah, Pedro de Corpa and four Franciscan companions, Spanish friars killed in 1597 on the Georgia coast, were beatified, Pope Leo noted at Sunday's Angelus. Cardinal Frank Leo of Toronto presided as papal legate. It was the first beatification in Georgia and in the American South, and the venue was expanded to hold about 12,000 people instead of 2,000 (OSV News; Catholic World Report). Church accounts say the friars were killed in part for upholding church teaching on marriage. At the All Saints' Angelus, Leo said "holiness is not a privilege reserved for a few." He hoped the Oleshky cooperation would lead to "new concrete peace initiatives" and asked prayers for all who suffer from war. He did not mention the US election, though Vatican News reported he'd voted by mail. Leo celebrates All Souls' Mass at Verano Cemetery this afternoon, Rome time, the first of 12 public Masses in November, nine of them on his Nov 6 trip to South America (NCR). His November prayer intention is the proper use of wealth.

Why it matters

The Savannah beatification gives the US church martyrs from its Spanish, pre-English roots, and the marriage theme lands days before the Court hears a Catholic school's religious-liberty case.

How each side sees it

Conservative Catholic press: martyrs for marriage

CNA and the Register stress that the friars died defending the indissolubility of marriage, a witness they present as pointed for today.

Via Catholic World Report / CNA (conservative)
Mainline and progressive Catholic press: a pastoral season

NCR and OSV frame the week around Leo's cemetery Mass, his crowded November and his prayer intention on wealth, with the martyrs as a milestone for the Southern church.

Via National Catholic Reporter (progressive) · OSV News (center) · Vatican.va (primary)
Watch: Leo's Verano homily today; the Nov 6 departure and survivor meetings on the trip.
