EscalatingIran war · day 222 (began Feb 28)

Iran's last barrels beyond the blockade are running out

What happened

Since Monday: a nonprofit cited by Bloomberg says dozens of loaded Iranian tankers are bottled up off Iran's coast. Iranian oil already stored beyond the blockade zone is down to about 10M barrels, from roughly 100M in July. At the current sales pace it could be gone by the end of October. No new crude has loaded at Iranian terminals since mid-August. The rial is around 2.69M to the dollar. On Tuesday VP Vance said Iran must make a "meaningful" cut to enrichment capacity to end the war, and questioned how much authority President Pezeshkian and FM Araghchi hold. Qatar's foreign ministry says mediation is "active." Interfax reports Pezeshkian will meet Putin in Turkmenistan on Friday. The independent newsletter Foreign Exchanges counts at least 13 attempted tanker attacks near Hormuz in about a week. Rubio said Iran has rejected "multiple" chances at a nuclear deal (Political Wire, Oct 7).

Why it matters

The blockade is close to cutting Iran's oil income to nothing. That raises the odds of either a deal or a sharper Iranian response at sea. Washington's demand now sits squarely on enrichment.

How each side sees it

The blockade is decisive

In Iran International, analyst Umud Shokri argues the crisis is "largely beyond the ministry's control." Swapping oil ministers can't reopen export routes, so economic pressure is the main lever.

Via Iran International (Persian diaspora, anti-regime) · Bloomberg
Stalemate view

Crisis Group's Ali Vaez, quoted by Foreign Exchanges, says neither side can reach its goals, yet "both feel continued fighting is worth it." Foreign Exchanges counts at least 13 attempted tanker attacks near Hormuz in about a week.

Via Foreign Exchanges (independent, restraint-leaning) · Restricted Handling
Watch: Friday's Pezeshkian–Putin meeting, and whether Iranian floating storage actually hits zero this month. Vance's and Qatar's remarks reached us through newsletter roundups citing Reuters; we didn't see the originals.

What happened

Monday night, Houthi attacks on two Saudi airports near the border wounded three people. EscalatingUkraine war & peace track

A birthday barrage for Putin; Europe answers with sanctions and money

What happened

Since Monday: Russia struck on Wednesday, Putin's 74th birthday, with about 70 missiles plus Shahed drones. At least 19 people were killed, 14 of them in Pryluky and 2 in Kyiv. The US, German, Italian, Spanish and Swedish embassies rejected Russia's demand that they leave Kyiv. Lithuania's parliament voted 106–18 to start lifting its constitutional ban on nuclear weapons, and the Kremlin promised countermeasures. Poland is moving Patriots toward its border. The Kyiv Independent reports that EU ambassadors approved the bloc's "largest-ever" sanctions list, that the EU set aside €1.2B for Ukrainian drones and missiles, and that Norway pledged about $9B for 2027; we saw only headlines on the sanctions. On the US-proposed trilateral talks, there was no date or venue as of Wednesday morning. The US license for Lukoil asset talks expires Oct 22, and Carlyle's ~$22B deal is still stuck.

How each side sees it

Kyiv: pressure, not concessions

Zelensky called the Pryluky strike a "birthday gift" to Putin, and he had warned Tuesday that a massive strike was coming. European capitals answered with sanctions, drone money and Norway's 2027 pledge.

Via Kyiv Independent · Kyiv Post
Low expectations on talks

The Kyiv Independent's sources say Trump privately doubts Putin will accept an energy truce before winter, and Ukrainian officials don't expect serious talks before spring. Disruption Banking notes the Lukoil sale remains locked by OFAC.

Via Kyiv Independent · Disruption Banking
Watch: the EU package's formal adoption, and whether Washington names a date for talks before the Lukoil license lapses Oct 22.

DevelopingEnergy crisis · Iran war day 222

Diesel finally falls, and the export-ban threat is gone

What happened

Since Monday: EIA's weekly survey put retail diesel at $6.199, down 18.3¢ and well off the $6.529 record. Gasoline fell 11¢ to $4.354. Trump dropped his threatened diesel-export ban. The G7 release now front-loads diesel within 20 days and spreads the rest over four months. OilPrice says diesel's premium over crude fell to about $69 from about $77. Brent slipped toward $100 Tuesday as Saudi exports held: PVM's John Evans puts them near 6M barrels a day via rerouting, with the East-West pipeline back at 5.8M. Saudi Aramco made an unexpected cut to November prices for Asia. Gulf-to-China supertanker rates are about $1.2M a day, against $80k a year ago.

Why it matters

It is a sizable one-week drop, 33¢ below the Sept 21 record. But a Dallas Fed survey finds 48% of oil executives expect diesel to take more than a year to get back to 2025 levels.

How each side sees it

"A band-aid"

Via OilPrice.com (energy trade)

DevelopingSCOTUS · October Term 2026

Boulder climate case: Barrett looks like the deciding vote

What happened

Since Monday's issue: in about two hours of argument before eight justices, Kavanaugh leaned toward the oil companies, warning such suits could "bankrupt" defendants. Kagan, Sotomayor and Jackson leaned toward Boulder. Kagan compared the case to tobacco and opioid litigation, and Jackson suggested it may be premature. Roberts and Gorsuch were mixed; Roberts asked whether the suit evades the Court's emissions precedents. Barrett called state tort law "a form of regulation," but pushed back on the companies' reading of the Clean Air Act's savings clause: "it doesn't say authorize; it says preserve." A 4–4 split would leave the Colorado ruling in place. The Trump administration urged dismissal.

How each side sees it

Via Daily Caller (right)

Via Balls & Strikes (progressive) · Stanford Law

401(k) suit looks doomed; Washington redistricting cases sent back

What happened

Several backed requiring a "meaningful benchmark" at the pleading stage, which would make such suits harder to bring. In Johnson v. Monday's order list had no new cases granted for full review. It sent back two Washington redistricting cases in light of Louisiana v. Callais, and a TPS case. The government's emergency request on transgender care in federal prisons is pending, with responses due Thursday. Guam's munitions-permit case is argued today.

How each side sees it

Via Supreme Court order list (primary) · PlanSponsor (trade)
Liberal justices

Sotomayor wrote about a capital case in which a judge had a late juror arrested in front of the jury pool. Jackson dissented from orders restricting filings by incarcerated petitioners. In Intel, Barrett worried a strict test makes courts "look for a twin."

Via Supreme Court order list · Law360 · Military.com

DevelopingRoad to COP31 · Antalya, Nov 9–20

Pacific leaders adopt a Tuvalu declaration and rail at slow climate finance

What happened

Since Monday: leaders adopted the "Taku Pakasoa Declaration" in Tuvalu. It calls for keeping 1.5°C within reach, more finance, and support for communities facing sea-level rise. Australia's PM's office dates its adoption to Oct 6, while Pacific press agency PINA says it still had to be signed at the Fiji plenary. Opening that plenary Wednesday, Fiji's PM Rabuka asked delegates to "see Tuvalu as the proxy" for judging their efforts. Ministers negotiate from Thursday. COP31 president Murat Kurum of Türkiye called the declaration "a call to unite" behind the Pacific. Closer to home: Tropical Storm Isaias (40 mph) is in the southwest Gulf moving east-northeast, and the NHC expects hurricane watches for part of the northern Gulf coast later today.

Skeptic of climate advocacy
