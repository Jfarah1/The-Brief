EscalatingIran war · day 255 (began Feb 28)

A "minor" US strike on Qeshm, a blockade runner disabled, and a softer Trump tone before today's Qatar meeting

What happened

Since Friday: the US carried out limited airstrikes on Iranian air defenses on Qeshm Island on Friday night, the first since the midterms. Trump called it a "minor" strike that took "about 15 minutes, maybe five," said "we are not fighting," and said his team had made "a lot of progress with Iran." Iranian state media said two missiles hit the Suza area of Qeshm; the IRGC claimed it downed a US drone, which the US hasn't confirmed (Euronews). On Saturday the US fired on and disabled a commercial ship it said was trying to breach the blockade of Iranian ports. Qatar delivered a two-day framework in which the US would take a set of steps on day one and Hormuz would reopen on day two; Iran's demands include lifting sanctions, cutting the US military presence, reinstating June's oil waivers, releasing frozen funds, recognizing a new Iran–Oman shipping lane, and ending the war on all fronts including Lebanon. On Sunday Trump said more targets, including 38 bridges, have been identified but new strikes are "highly unlikely" if talks produce a strong deal. Rubio said Iran's demands are "not just unacceptable" and in some cases unrealistic, and Axios reports CENTCOM is ready to resume major operations if ordered. Trump and Vance meet Qatari PM Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani today (Al Jazeera).

Why it matters

Both sides are signaling they can wait, but a deal on Hormuz is the single biggest swing factor for fuel prices, and today's meeting is the first face-to-face test of Qatar's plan.

How each side sees it

Tehran: meet the conditions, or face "hell"

Foreign Minister Araghchi said Iran will "rain down hell" if the US resumes attacks and wants "regional security arrangements without the interference of superregional powers." SNSC secretary Rezaei: "Start war and you will face a devastating response."

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded) · Euronews (European)
Washington: talks, with targets ready

Trump frames the strikes as small and defensive and says Iran wants a deal; Rubio says there's no breakthrough and the US will act if shipping or forces are threatened.

Via CNBC (mainstream) · Al Jazeera (citing Axios)
Israeli analyst: Tehran is preparing for a longer war

Former Israeli military intelligence officer Danny Citrinowicz says Tehran isn't intimidated and would likely widen any new war to Gulf states from the start, while Washington may be planning for a short one.

Via Euronews
⚠ We found no restraint-camp or hawkish think-tank commentary dated this cycle; the US position comes from officials' own remarks.

DevelopingIsraeli election · voted Oct 27

Eisenkot gets the mandate and promises a "national emergency government"; Netanyahu warns of "national suicide"

What happened

Since Friday: on Sunday President Herzog formally tasked Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming a government, citing 67 recommendations to Netanyahu's 46 (JTA). Eisenkot has 28 days, extendable by 14. He said he'll try to build a national emergency government even if it takes "creative and unconventional political initiatives," and set conditions for partners, including support for Israel as a Jewish and democratic state and treating Hamas as a terrorist group (NBC). He was due to meet Naftali Bennett Sunday and Avigdor Liberman after that. Netanyahu remains prime minister until a new government is sworn in, and the new Knesset is sworn in Tuesday.

Why it matters

Eisenkot's numbers depend on Arab-led lists, at least from outside the coalition. His conditions look designed to keep Liberman and the center-right on board while leaving room for that outside support.

How each side sees it

The change bloc: a clear majority for a new government

Eisenkot's camp says 67 recommendations are an absolute majority and the country wants an emergency unity government after the war years.

Via JTA (Jewish news agency, center) · ANI (Indian agency)
Netanyahu and the right: a government resting on Arab parties is dangerous

Netanyahu called Arab support for an Eisenkot government potential "national suicide"; the settler-right press frames the mandate as a handover to the left.

Via CBN News (US Christian, right) · Arutz Sheva (Israeli, right/settler)
Watch: Liberman's terms; whether Ra'am or the Joint List are offered anything formal; Tuesday's Knesset swearing-in.

EscalatingUkraine war / peace track

Russia's biggest strike on the Kyiv region in weeks hits power and a major bridge, two days before Abu Dhabi

What happened

Since Friday: overnight into Saturday Russia launched 172 drones and 36 ballistic missiles, Zelensky said; Ukraine's air force downed 149 drones but only four missiles. One person was killed and eight wounded, including a child. Kyiv's mayor said power, heating and water were in an "extremely difficult" state, and the Northern Bridge was damaged; Russia's defense ministry said it hit military-industrial, energy and transport targets (The Moscow Times). Over the weekend a Ukrainian drone strike set fire to Russia's Kirishi refinery (Business Recorder, citing Reuters). The talks in Abu Dhabi start Tuesday: Russia is sending Vladimir Medinsky plus deputy foreign ministers Ryabkov and Galuzin, military-intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov and a General Staff officer, a higher-level team than before (Kyiv Post). Rustem Umerov leads Ukraine's side, a senior official confirmed (Kyiv Independent). These are the first three-way talks since February.

Why it matters

Russia is hitting the grid hard as winter starts, which raises the cost of saying no in Abu Dhabi. A larger Russian military delegation hints the talks could get into ceasefire mechanics, not just statements.

How each side sees it

Kyiv: strikes as negotiating pressure

Zelensky tied the weekend attack to Moscow's preparations for the talks, arguing Russia bombs before it negotiates. Kyiv media note Medinsky's return raised doubts about a breakthrough.

Via The Moscow Times (exile, anti-Kremlin) · Kyiv Post (Ukrainian)
Moscow: security guarantees, behind closed doors

The Kremlin says the talks will focus on security guarantees, discussed privately, and has ruled out European participation despite France saying it wants in.

Via TASS (Russian state media) · Ukrainska Pravda (Ukrainian)
Watch: Whether a partial energy truce is on the table Tuesday; Putin's APEC plans for Nov 18–19.

EscalatingSaudi–Houthi war · since July

Missiles over Riyadh and debris on an Aramco building, as Houthis say Saudi strikes killed 33 near Sanaa

What happened

Since Friday: on Sunday the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two Houthi ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh after loud booms in the capital, then two cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh and the south. Civil Defence issued an alert shortly before. Falling shrapnel hit an Aramco administrative building; the Energy Ministry called the damage minor, with no injuries (The National). Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed "six operations" against targets including King Khalid International Airport, Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais, a pumping station at Dawadmi and Abqaiq; a later statement said four, and none is verified. A day earlier, Houthi-run health authorities said Saudi airstrikes near Sanaa killed at least 33 people; Saudi authorities say they're investigating. The coalition says it destroyed a launch platform in Sanaa (Jerusalem Post). We found no update on the reported $51 billion Houthi demand.

Why it matters

Attacks on the capital and on Aramco put Saudi oil infrastructure back in play just as markets were pricing a calmer Gulf.

How each side sees it

Riyadh and Gulf press: defenses held

Saudi officials stress the interceptions and minor damage, and the coalition says it is hitting launchers at the source.

Via The National (UAE, government-aligned) · Jerusalem Post (Israeli, center-right)
Houthis: hitting the oil economy, while Saudi bombs kill civilians

The Houthis say they're targeting airports and oil sites and that Saudi strikes are killing civilians around Sanaa.

NewFed / labor market

October brings 165,000 jobs, nearly triple forecasts, and the December hike is back on the table; chips bounce

What happened

Since Friday: the Labor Department said payrolls rose 165,000 in October against forecasts of about 59,000–65,000; private employers added 159,000. August and September were revised up a combined 84,000, and unemployment fell to 4.1% from 4.2% (BLS; CNBC). CME FedWatch put December hike odds at about 77–79%, up from about 67% a week earlier (Morningstar). The 10-year yield rose to 4.86% and the dollar firmed (ETF Trends). Stocks were mixed: the S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,376.86 and the Nasdaq 1.1%, while the Dow fell about 0.5%. The chip index bounced about 4% after Thursday's 12% drop, and AMD rose more than 11% (StockTitan). The S&P 500 still had its worst week since June. October CPI comes Tuesday.

Why it matters

Last week's case for a Fed pause rested on a cooling job market. That case is weaker now, and a hike would land on an economy already absorbing the AI selloff and war-driven fuel prices.

How each side sees it

Hawks: the Fed has its excuse

Morningstar's Preston Caldwell says the Fed will be "more eager to hike rates in December" after the report. Allianz Trade's Dan North found it hard to find anything to dislike.

Via Morningstar (investment research) · CNBC (mainstream)
Skeptics: not a game-changer

Axios notes bond and futures markets barely shifted, suggesting the report moved odds without settling the debate; falling oil and tech stocks could still argue against tightening.

Via Axios (mainstream) · BLS (primary)

What happened

DevelopingSCOTUS October Term 2026

The Court hears whether a federal prisoner can sue a prison nurse, the next test for Bivens suits

What happened

Quiet in the courts this cycle, with the Court's orders list at 9 AM CT today and one big argument after it. Nielsen v. Watanabe asks whether federal inmate Ketei Watanabe can sue prison nurse Francis Nielsen for damages under the Eighth Amendment. He says that after a 2021 assault in a Honolulu prison he was given over-the-counter painkillers and denied outside care for years; an x-ray later showed a fractured coccyx. The Ninth Circuit let the suit proceed. Nielsen, backed by the federal government, argues the claim fails the Court's "demanding two-part test" for extending Bivens and that the prison grievance system is an adequate alternative (SCOTUSblog; The New Republic). Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission, on pipeline landowners' fees, follows. We found no new ruling in the Washington or Alaska ballot cases.

Why it matters

Bivens is the main way to sue federal officers for constitutional violations. A ruling against Watanabe could close off one of the last remaining paths, which matters for immigration and prison cases alike.

How each side sees it

Progressive legal press: an "empire of impunity"

The New Republic's Matt Ford argued in June that the Roberts Court's approach to power can be summed up as "impunity." The Constitutional Accountability Center backs Watanabe.

Via The New Republic (left) · Constitutional Accountability Center (progressive)
The government: Congress, not courts, should create damages remedies

Nielsen and the Solicitor General argue Carlson v. Green covered far worse misconduct and shouldn't be stretched, and that alternative remedies count against a new claim.

Via SCOTUSblog (nonpartisan)
⚠ We found no conservative legal commentary on the case dated this cycle; the government's side comes from its own briefs as summarized by SCOTUSblog.

DevelopingCOP31 · Antalya, Nov 9–20

COP31 opens with weaker national pledges, fewer leaders and an oil refiner as top sponsor

What happened

COP31 opens today at the Antalya Expo Center, with Turkish environment minister Murat Kurum as president and Australia's Chris Bowen leading negotiations; day one focuses on food, agriculture and health, and the leaders' summit is Nov 11–12 (bianet). Azerbaijani outlets report 102 heads of state or government and 300-plus ministers confirmed (Azernews), but only 77 leaders are on the provisional speakers' list, against 116 at COP28. The UN finds current national plans would cut emissions about 6% below 2019 levels by 2035, or 9% with conditional pledges, down from a 12% estimate last year, in large part because of the US exit from Paris. The first loss-and-damage payout is $20 million for Nepal, against $2.69 billion in losses. The top corporate sponsor is Koç Holding, whose energy arm is dominated by refiner Tüpraş (Climate and Capital Media). The US has no official delegation.

Why it matters

With pledges slipping and the US absent, Antalya is mostly about adaptation money and keeping the process credible, not about new emissions targets.

How each side sees it

The host: an "implementation COP" and a new era

Kurum frames Antalya as the start of a new era of climate cooperation focused on delivering on existing commitments; Turkish state broadcaster TRT stresses calls for "more decisive action."

Via Hürriyet Daily News (Turkish, center) · TRT World (Turkish state media)
Climate critics: the wrong signal

Former UN climate chief Yvo de Boer calls the low turnout "the wrong signal at a difficult time," and critics question how a refiner-linked sponsor passed the UN's integrity check.

Via Climate and Capital Media (climate finance) · APA (Azerbaijani, state-aligned agency)
⚠ We found no climate-skeptic or ecomodernist commentary on COP31 dated this cycle.

DevelopingLeo's pontificate · Latin America trip Nov 6–17

After Uruguay, Leo reaches Argentina and urges Milei and other leaders to "safeguard democracy"

What happened

Since Friday: in his first address in Montevideo, Leo pointed to his own recent illness and care and called universal health care a duty "in every society" (America), while also urging protection of life at all stages in the country that legalized euthanasia in April (EWTN). On Saturday he said Mass for about 40,000 in Paysandú, met students at the Jesuit-run Catholic University and led a vigil for about 70,000 young people at Centenario Stadium. On Sunday he landed in Buenos Aires, where President Milei met him on the tarmac before a private meeting at the Casa Rosada, the first papal visit since 1987 (Buenos Aires Herald). Speaking to authorities, he urged leaders to protect democracy, respect the unborn and treat opponents as fellow citizens, said "My arrival in this land is personally meaningful," and asked why young people feel they must emigrate (OSV News via Catholic Standard). Today: an outdoor Mass in central Buenos Aires, then social movements, religious leaders and the local church. He moves to Peru Wednesday.

Why it matters

Milei's austerity has been criticized by the Argentine bishops, and both he and Leo have disagreed with Trump on migrants and the Iran war. Leo's remarks in Francis's home country set the tone for the rest of the trip.

How each side sees it

Conservative Catholic press: life at every stage

EWTN pairs Leo's health-care appeal with his defense of life "at all stages," reading it as a rebuke of Uruguay's euthanasia law.

Via EWTN News (conservative) · Catholic Standard / OSV News (diocesan, center)
Jesuit and center-left press: health care and "creative courage"

America leads with universal health care and Leo's call for "creative courage" in evangelizing secular Uruguay; CNN frames the Argentina message as aimed at Milei, a Trump ally.

Via America (center-left, Jesuit) · CNN (mainstream) · Vatican News (official)
