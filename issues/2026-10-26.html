NewBrazil election · runoff Oct 25

Lula wins a fourth term by about 4.4 million votes; Flávio Bolsonaro refuses to accept it

What happened

Since Friday: with all votes counted, the electoral court (TSE) has Lula at about 51.8% and Flávio Bolsonaro at about 48.2%, roughly 64.3 million votes to 59.8 million. It is Lula's fourth term, and the first time since 1989 that the second-place finisher in the first round won the runoff (Flávio led Oct 4, 47.0% to 45.2%). The dispute is over closing time. Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin extended voting by two hours after Lula's campaign cited problems with buses carrying voters; Flávio's campaign had also asked for more time, citing rain in the south. Some polling stations learned of the order only after they had closed. Flávio called the late rule change "unacceptable," said recognizing the result is "impossible," and is seeking annulment. TSE president Kassio Nunes Marques, a Bolsonaro appointee, said the extension did not change the outcome (TSE; Bloomberg Línea; AP via CBS). Uruguay, Paraguay, Bolivia and Guyana congratulated Lula, and the OAS urged respect for the result. We found no reaction yet from Trump, who called the first round "a great victory" for Flávio. Bloomberg Línea reported a sharp after-hours sell-off in Brazilian assets; Monday's open will show how deep it goes.

Why it matters

Latin America's largest economy now has a sitting president and a challenger disputing the count, three years after the Jan. 8 riots. How Washington responds will matter: the Trump administration openly favored Flávio.

How each side sees it

Lula and the court: the result stands

Lula said he must "govern for 215 million" Brazilians and promised to regulate social media platforms. The TSE says both campaigns asked for extensions and the extra two hours did not decide the race.

Via TSE (official) · Bloomberg Línea (Latin American business)
Bolsonaro camp: a judge changed the rules mid-game

Campaign coordinator Rogério Marinho called Zanin's order "shameful and untimely." Senator-elect Marcel van Hattem pledged an impeachment request against the justice.

Via Bloomberg Línea (quoting the PL campaign) · CBS News (mainstream, AP reporting)
Watch: A formal PL challenge at the TSE; any Trump or Rubio statement; street mobilization by Bolsonaro supporters; the real and Brazilian stocks Monday. Inauguration is Jan 5.

EscalatingVoting-law rulings · since Oct 1

A rare Sunday hearing on 20,000 California ballots, and DOJ sues over Alaska's Senate count

What happened

Since Friday: the Ninth Circuit held an unusual Sunday argument on DOJ's bid to make California set aside the mail ballots of about 20,000 people it calls "likely noncitizens." Judges Bennett (a Trump appointee) and Sanchez (a Biden appointee) sounded skeptical on jurisdiction and the merits; Judge Bumatay was more receptive. No ruling yet, and California says it will seek full-court review if it loses (Election Law Blog, Law Dork). Separately, DOJ sued Oct 22 to block certification of Alaska's Senate race if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses. Its theory is that a second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, confused voters to Mary Peltola's benefit. Trump said "I'm the one that said 'do it.'" On Sunday, Attorney General Todd Blanche said DOJ "does not seek to stop" certification, only to preserve ballots. Sen. Lisa Murkowski said the suit should never have been filed, and Sullivan's campaign says it didn't ask for it (Alaska Beacon, CNBC). A motion to dismiss is pending before Judge Sharon Gleason.

Why it matters
