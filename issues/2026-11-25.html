NewUS AI policy · chips and China

Trump gets Huang reinstated at Nvidia; China hawks in his own party object

What happened

Since Monday: Nvidia's board voted unanimously Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, effective immediately. He resigned Nov 12 after reports of affairs, one of them with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore firm suspected of diverting chips to China. CNBC reports that senior administration officials told the board Huang hadn't broken national-security laws. Trump said "I convinced them" and "I made the deal." Nvidia rose 31.5% Monday, adding about $950 billion in value, then fell about 6% Tuesday. The same day, Commerce added Megaspeed and six affiliates in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to the Entity List, a move Semafor reported first. Tuesday, Attorney General Todd Blanche called Nvidia a "good corporate citizen," and Trump told the New York Post a pardon for Huang is "always an option" (NBC live blog). Treasury's deadline for Nvidia to answer on shell companies is today. On Nov 22 Bessent said Treasury didn't have the answers yet. We found no report of a reply. Huang faces a closed-door House interview Dec 1.

Why it matters

The White House overrode the export-control questions its own Treasury secretary raised a week ago. That moves the fight over chip leaks to China into Congress, where the hawks are Republicans and Democrats take the gavels in January.

How each side sees it

Via CNBC (mainstream) · NBC News (mainstream)

Via CNN via KRDO (mainstream) · Semafor (center)
Democrats and skeptics: a political rescue

NewExecutive actions

Trump orders DOJ to seek and speed federal death sentences

What happened

Since Monday: in a presidential memorandum Monday, Trump directed the attorney general to seek the death penalty for first-degree murder of a child and for murders committed during rape, child sexual exploitation, kidnapping or trafficking. It also tells DOJ to move faster on executions: prioritize appeals and habeas cases, oppose "dilatory" tactics and seek expedited court review. The text says "a prolonged delay in carrying out a death sentence is a grave offense" (whitehouse.gov). It applies only to federal cases. It follows California Gov. Newsom's commutation of all 548 state death sentences. Blanche says DOJ has sought death 49 times under Trump, against once under Biden. It is a memorandum, not an executive order, and it isn't in the Federal Register yet. It was the only new presidential action listed on whitehouse.gov in the window.

Why it matters

Federal executions depend on prosecutors' charging choices and on the courts' timelines. The memo pushes both, and it sets up a clash with states like California that have stepped away from capital punishment.

How each side sees it

Supporters: justice for child victims

Conservative outlets frame it as ordering DOJ to pursue "child killers and murderous traffickers." Trump says punishment "will be swift and certain."

Via WLT Report (right)
Critics: speed kills the innocent

HuffPost cites at least 200 death-row exonerations. Newsom: "If you're poor, Black or brown, you get the needle."

Via HuffPost (left)
Watch: New federal capital notices; DOJ motions to speed up pending appeals.

NewTrump's criminal cases

Appeals court revives Trump's bid to move his hush-money conviction to federal court

What happened

Monday, a 2nd Circuit panel (Lohier, Walker, Cabranes) reversed the late Judge Alvin Hellerstein in a two-page order. Hellerstein had refused Trump's second attempt to move the case. The panel held the district court "erred in holding that removal is categorically unavailable in a state criminal case" after conviction, and found good cause for the late filing. A fuller opinion will follow. The case goes back to a Manhattan federal judge, who must rule "promptly." The panel paused Trump's state appeal to preserve the status quo. The conviction still stands. Trump will argue prosecutors used official-acts evidence barred by the 2024 immunity ruling (Courthouse News; Al Jazeera). His team praised a "prompt and correct ruling." The Manhattan DA's office declined to comment.

Why it matters

A federal forum is Trump's best path to vacating the only criminal conviction of a US president. Pausing the state appeal also delays that route to a ruling.

NewExecutive power · sanctions

Judge permanently blocks the ICC sanctions order's "services" ban, nationwide

What happened

Monday, Judge Jesse Furman (S.D.N.Y.) ruled in DAWN v. Trump that the "services provision" of the ICC sanctions executive order violates the First Amendment. The provision penalizes providing services to the International Criminal Court, UN rapporteur Francesca Albanese and three Palestinian NGOs. He made his October preliminary injunction permanent and extended it to all Americans, not just the plaintiffs (The Intercept). DAWN's Michael Omer-Man: "the universal part is huge." The government had argued the order regulates conduct, not speech. The White House didn't comment. An appeal is likely.

Why it matters

It is a nationwide remedy after the Supreme Court sharply limited universal injunctions in Trump v. CASA (2025), so the scope itself is likely to be appealed.

How each side sees it

Plaintiffs: speech can't be sanctioned

Human-rights groups say the order chilled lawyers and researchers from working with the court at all.

Via The Intercept (left) · Just Security (center-left)
Government: foreign-policy conduct, not speech

In its filings, the administration argued the order targets services to a foreign body and doesn't restrict expression.

Via The Intercept, summarizing government filings

Supreme Court defers the mootness fight in the assault-weapons case; the arch goes to the 4th Circuit

Since Monday: the Court let lead plaintiff Cutberto Viramontes withdraw, so the Cook County AR-15 case is now Khaya v. Cook County. Tuesday's order list, its only order, deferred the county's request to dismiss the case as moot "pending oral argument" (supremecourt.gov; SCOTUSblog). The county says the new lead plaintiff wants an IMI Galil, not an AR-15-style rifle. It is argued Dec 2 alongside Connecticut's ban (Grant v. Higgins). There was no other action, including on the EPA climate-grant petition. On the arch: DOJ appealed Judge Brinkema's injunction to the 4th Circuit and says it will seek a stay (Washington Times). DC, Virginia and veterans' groups asked Judge Chutkan Monday to issue her own injunction anyway, to avoid a "gap" if the 4th Circuit grants a stay (Law&Crime). DOJ called an injunction "particularly damaging" and cited the arch's national-security benefits. Chutkan's TRO runs to Dec 3.

Why it matters

The gun case is the Court's first major test of assault-weapons bans since Bruen, so the mootness question decides whether it rules at all. The arch is now a test of how far courts will police a presidential monument.
