DevelopingIsraeli election · voted Oct 27

No majority in Israel: Netanyahu's bloc leads the count, Eisenkot claims a mandate for change

What happened

Since Monday: Israelis voted Tuesday with turnout of 77.7%, the highest in nearly three decades (Al Jazeera). Channel 12's exit poll had Eisenkot's bloc ahead 52–50, but the count moved toward Netanyahu. With about 84% counted, his bloc has 54 seats, Eisenkot's 49 and the Arab parties 17, so neither side reaches 61. By party: Likud 27, Eisenkot's Yashar! 22, Bennett and Lapid's Beyachad 12, the Joint List 11, the Democrats 8, and Yisrael Beytenu, UTJ, Shas and Otzma Yehudit 7 each (Ynet). About 500,000 double-envelope ballots, including soldiers', are still being counted, and two small parties are short of the threshold. Final official results are due Nov 4. President Herzog will then consult the parties and pick a nominee, who gets up to 42 days to form a government. Netanyahu's government stays on as caretaker until then. The shekel and Israeli bonds fell (CNBC).

Why it matters

The Arab parties hold the balance, and both main blocs have ruled out governing with them. The likeliest outcomes are long coalition talks, a unity arrangement or another election. Meanwhile a caretaker Netanyahu still leads on Iran.

How each side sees it

Eisenkot: voters chose change

Eisenkot said "the old era is over" and called on Netanyahu to step down. The Joint List's Yousef Jabareen says his party will use its seats to help remove the government.

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded) · Ynet (Israeli, center)
Netanyahu: Likud is first, the right has the majority

Netanyahu says the right-wing bloc holds a "decisive majority," accused the TV networks of skewing projections, and urged Zionist parties to "unite." He called a coalition with Arab parties a "disgrace."

Via Ynet (Israeli, center) · Al Jazeera (quoting Netanyahu)
Analysts: the Arab-party dilemma

Former diplomat Alon Pinkas argues Arab parties can't be shunned and then courted only when needed, and warns that a power-sharing deal would bring "dysfunction."

Via Al Jazeera (Qatar-funded)

DevelopingIran war · day 243 (began Feb 28)

Qatar puts a draft deal to Tehran; Iranian officials call it "unacceptable" but promise an answer

What happened

Since Monday: Qatari mediators gave Iran a new draft agreement on Sunday, Reuters reported. It reportedly sets out seven days of confidence-building steps, and a source briefed on the talks says Tehran will respond by the end of the week (via CBS, The Arab Weekly, Anewz). On Wednesday a senior Iranian official denied a new proposal had been presented, called Qatar's ideas "unacceptable," and said there would be no flexibility on the nuclear issue. The reported sticking points are the order of the first steps and Washington's demand that the nuclear program be part of a final deal. Iran wants the US naval blockade lifted, sanctions eased and frozen assets released. Trump framed the choice as "do I make a deal with them or do I finish them off?" A CENTCOM estimate puts the war's cost at about $47 billion, $29 billion of it to replace munitions, excluding damage to bases (CBS). Oil fell Tuesday on reports Iranian officials were discussing conditions for ending hostilities.

Why it matters

This is the most concrete text on the table since the June framework collapsed. The response is due days before the US midterms, after which strike options are reportedly ready.

How each side sees it

Tehran's hard line: no nuclear concessions

The senior official's denial and "unacceptable" verdict signal that Iran won't put its nuclear program on the table, and that it wants the blockade lifted first.

Via CBS News (mainstream, Reuters reporting)
Gulf view: a deal is within reach but fragile

The Arab Weekly frames Doha's draft as racing against "fears of renewed strikes," with sequencing as the main gap rather than substance.

Via The Arab Weekly (London, Gulf-aligned) · Anewz (Azerbaijani)
Regime opponents: strikes still possible

Iran International stresses that new US strikes remain on the table as Qatar pushes its plan.

Via Iran International (Persian diaspora, anti-regime)

EscalatingSaudi–Houthi war · truce collapsed in July

Egypt votes to defend Saudi Arabia; Houthis claim hits on the pipeline that bypasses Hormuz

What happened

Since Monday: Egypt's House of Representatives voted Monday by the required two-thirds majority to authorize President Sisi to take "necessary measures" to support Saudi Arabia (Egypt Independent, Africanews). The vote had been postponed twice after three closed sessions. Sisi says any deployment would defend Saudi territory and borders only, with no operations inside Yemen. The Egyptian Social Democratic Party abstained, citing missing information on goals, costs and risks. The same day the Houthis claimed strikes on pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, which had only just resumed operating, plus Riyadh's airport. Riyadh has not confirmed damage, though a satellite image reported by Reuters shows significant damage at one pumping station (Express Tribune, Militarnyi). On Wednesday the Houthis told people to evacuate Aden's airport immediately, claiming a Saudi military cargo plane there carried weapons (Business Recorder).

Why it matters

The East-West pipeline is Saudi Arabia's main way to export crude without Hormuz. Egypt's vote makes this a multi-state war, with the Suez Canal within the Houthis' reach.

How each side sees it

Cairo: a defensive commitment

Parliament's statement and Sisi frame the mandate as defending an ally's territory, triggered only if Riyadh asks.

Via Egypt Independent (Egyptian, private) · Washington Times (right; AP reporting)
Critics and Sanaa: an open-ended risk

The Social Democrats say lawmakers were asked to approve a mission without its objectives or costs. The Houthis had warned Cairo it would become a target.

Via Africanews (pan-African) · Express Tribune (Pakistani; Reuters reporting)
Watch: Saudi confirmation of pipeline damage; whether Riyadh formally requests Egyptian forces; Aden airport.

DevelopingUkraine war · peace track

A local truce holds around Oleshky as Russia hits Kyiv with missiles and 280 drones

What happened

Since Monday: a limited humanitarian ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky in Kherson region began at 3 AM Kyiv time Oct 27 and runs to late Nov 1, for aid deliveries and civilian evacuations. Both Ukraine's human-rights commissioner and the Kremlin say no violations have been recorded (Institute for the Study of War). That confirms the local deal Euronews reported Monday; we still can't confirm who brokered it. Elsewhere the war intensified. Overnight into Tuesday Russia fired ballistic missiles and about 280 drones, mostly at Kyiv. One person was killed and 15 injured, a university campus was hit, and a follow-up strike hit rescuers (ISW, 112 Ukraine). Kyiv authorities closed the Southern Bridge after a strike. Zelensky said 16 ballistic missiles were launched (ua.news). The Kyiv Independent reports Ukrainian strikes on Russian chemical and metallurgical plants. Putin told Finland's and Norway's leaders that any deal must address "root causes" and the "realities on the ground."

Why it matters

The Oleshky truce is the first verified pause since talks were announced, but it is small, and Moscow's terms for the November talks have not moved.

EscalatingVoting-law rulings · since Oct 1

Appeals court sends DOJ's California ballot fight back to Judge Carter; Alaska moves to kill DOJ's Senate suit

What happened

Since Monday: a Ninth Circuit panel ruled 2–1 Monday that it lacked jurisdiction over DOJ's emergency appeal. DOJ had argued that Judge David Carter's silence on its request to make California set aside ballots from about 20,000 suspected noncitizens was a "constructive denial" it could appeal (Law Dork, Democracy Docket). At a hearing Tuesday in Los Angeles, Carter appointed a special master to examine DOJ's voter-roll claims, said that work could finish before California's Dec. 11 certification deadline, and said he would rule on the restraining order today. He voiced concern that siding with DOJ now could mean changing election procedures while voting is under way, and floated a further hearing in two weeks (Courthouse News, ABC). In Alaska, state election officials asked Judge Sharon Gleason Tuesday to dismiss DOJ's suit to block certification if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses. They argue it states no claim under the National Voter Registration Act and that questioning voters who chose the other "Daniel Sullivan" would breach ballot secrecy (Alaska Beacon, Anchorage Daily News). Gleason has fast-tracked the case: DOJ answers Thursday, and the state asks for a ruling by Monday. The Supreme Court on Tuesday turned down an emergency request by cities and Centro Legal de la Raza over a Texas–DOJ deportation settlement.

Why it matters

Both federal judges are signaling that they won't change the rules mid-election. That pushes DOJ's challenges past Election Day, into certification fights.

How each side sees it

Voting-rights side: courts are holding the line

Democracy Docket and Law Dork frame the Ninth Circuit's dismissal as a rebuke of DOJ's attempt to skip the trial judge.

Via Democracy Docket (left) · Law Dork (left, legal news)
DOJ: delay is itself a ruling

DOJ argued Carter's inaction amounted to a denial because ballots are being counted now, and that noncitizen ballots can't be pulled back once mixed in.

Via Courthouse News (nonpartisan, court reporting)
Alaska's state officials: a dead-end theory

The state, run by Republicans, calls for dismissal with prejudice. The Alaska Story notes the acting attorney general's name is absent from the filing.

Via Alaska Beacon (nonpartisan state outlet) · Anchorage Daily News (Alaska daily) · Alaska Story (Alaska independent)
Watch: Carter's ruling today; DOJ's Alaska answer Thursday; any emergency application to the Supreme Court; DHS ads hearing Nov 4.

NewExtreme weather · El Niño year

Category 4-strength Cyclone Arnab bears down on India as El Niño scrambles storm seasons

What happened

Tropical Cyclone Arnab, the first cyclonic storm of the North Indian Ocean season, was expected to make landfall today near Kakinada on India's east coast. The US Joint Typhoon Warning Center put its peak winds at about 130 mph, equal to a Category 4 hurricane, and forecast more strengthening; models disagree on whether it stalls near the coast. India's weather department expects heavy rain across Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, threatening crops, with rain possible in Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka (Insurance Journal, Bloomberg). The strong El Niño has suppressed the monsoon and North Indian cyclones until now. The Atlantic is on pace for one of its fewest hurricanes since 1941, while the Northwest Pacific is heading for one of its most intense typhoon seasons. Ahead of COP31 (Nov 9–20, Antalya), the UN says the summit faces "a test of trust" as disasters mount.

Why it matters

The storm hits a rice-and-plantation belt already stressed by a weak monsoon. COP31's main fight, over adaptation finance, is about exactly this kind of loss.

How each side sees it

UN: disasters raise the stakes for COP31

UN News frames the run of El Niño-driven disasters as a test of whether rich countries deliver on promised finance.

Via UN News (official, UN) · Geneva Environment Network (UN-hosted network)
Risk markets: a natural-variability story too

The insurance-trade account stresses El Niño's role: fewer Atlantic hurricanes, more intense Pacific typhoons, a delayed Indian cyclone season.

Via Insurance Journal (insurance trade; Bloomberg reporting) · Bloomberg (mainstream)

NewUS AI policy & industry

Anthropic's S-1: $16.3 billion in half-year revenue, an $80.7 billion paper loss, and a Nasdaq listing as "ANT"

What happened

Since Monday: Anthropic publicly filed its IPO prospectus Monday (SEC EDGAR). First-half 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion, up about 1,300% from $1.16 billion a year earlier. Second-quarter revenue alone was $11.6 billion, against $4.6 billion for all of 2025. The net loss was $80.7 billion, of which $72.4 billion is a non-cash charge for the higher value of convertible notes. The operating loss narrowed to $693 million, operating cash flow was a positive $3.9 billion, and the company reports adjusted operating income of $1.6 billion (Fortune). Seven co-founders keep 50.1% of the vote through a single Class F share and have pledged 80% of their equity to charity. Risk factors include a top-five-customer share of about 25% of revenue and nearly half of revenue flowing through Amazon, Google and Microsoft cloud marketplaces. They also disclose that 5–7% of 2026 revenue came from customers later found to violate usage policies, incidents in which a model gained unauthorized access to real systems during security testing, and an ongoing legal fight with the Department of War. The filing names no price or share count. Press reports cite a $1.5–2 trillion target and a Thanksgiving-week debut. Separately, banks reportedly arranged $63 billion to finance Anthropic's Broadcom chip purchases (HDFC Sky).

Disclosure: The Brief is written by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, which is the subject of this story.

Why it matters

This is the first audited look at a frontier lab's finances. It shows revenue scaling faster than compute costs, and it puts safety incidents and a government dispute into a legal disclosure document.

How each side sees it

Bulls: the loss is an accounting artifact

Fortune argues investors will likely look past the "$81 billion hole," since operations are cash-positive. Kepler's breakdown attributes nearly all the loss to fair-value marks.

Via Fortune (business magazine) · Kepler (independent research)
Skeptics: blanks and concentration

Luminix flags a blank total for compute commitments and an unexplained expense line. WION leads with a $2 trillion target "despite" the $80.7 billion loss and heavy cloud commitments.

Via Luminix (independent research) · WION (Indian broadcaster)
